# Black Metal — ChatGPT Project Prompts

Copy one prompt into the Black Metal project and replace bracketed fields.

## Research add-on

```text
Browse as of today; open sources before relying on them. Verify identity, original year, edition, format, and release status. Cite direct sources near claims. Distinguish fact, classification, promotional copy, editorial recommendation, and my listening feedback. Do not invent listening, scores, consensus, or artist affiliations.
```

## 1. Start the project

```text
Read the uploaded sources and summarize the project’s scope and gaps. Offer chronological and sound-based listening paths, then three contrasting albums. Ask what I already enjoy; do not assume listens or scores.
```

## 2. Whole history, in chapters

```text
Build a sourced chapter plan from precursors/first wave through Norwegian second wave, international parallel scenes, and the 2000s–present. Explain musical changes, labels, production, distribution, and cultural context. Start with the first chapter, not a claim that a short overview is the whole history. Compare disputed accounts.
```

## 3. Styles and listening cues

```text
Map the major Black Metal styles and hybrids with practical sonic cues, two verified representative albums per style, and boundary discussions. Separate scene, theme, raw production, and ideological labels from sonic branches. Explain neighboring Viking metal, dungeon synth, war metal, and blackgaze.
```

## 4. Classic albums

```text
Recommend five classic or overlooked albums for [era/style]. Verify original years and editions, explain historical importance separately from musical appeal, and give direct sources plus official listening links or clearly labeled searches. Include contrasts rather than only famous Norwegian records.
```

## 5. First versus second wave

```text
Compare first-wave recordings with the early-1990s second wave. Explain changes in riffs, vocals, drums, composition, production, and distribution through three album pairs with sourced original years. Distinguish influence claims from disputed origin stories.
```

## 6. Regional scene

```text
Research [country/region, for example Chile, Greece, Finland, Czechia, or Japan]. Explain documented local history, key recordings, distinctive approaches where supported, and contemporary discoveries. Do not assume every local band shares one sound or ideology.
```

## 7. Rank one year

```text
Build a reasoned top [five/ten] Black Metal album list for [year]. Verify original studio-full-length eligibility and state the candidate pool, hybrid policy, criteria, sources, and gaps. Separate editorial order, published rankings, and my favorites. Do not pad thin pools, invent scores, or rank reissues as new originals.
```

## 8. Develop the annual archive

```text
Work through [year range] one year at a time. Build verified candidate pools before ordering albums; compare published lists by named author/audience. Track research status and missing scenes. Provide concise Markdown for the annual archive and explain why each provisional placement could change.
```

## 9. Current-year releases

```text
Search the actual current calendar year through today and state the cutoff. Verify responsible artist/label dates and released-full-album status. Separate full-lengths, upcoming records, EPs/demos/splits, and reissues. Include independent releases and varied scenes. Recommend three discovery priorities without ranking an unavailable album.
```

## 10. Emerging bands

```text
Find debut/early-discography Black Metal projects in [year/region/style]. Verify debut and formation information where possible. Distinguish new projects with experienced members from established acts. Give entry recordings, evidence, and independent appraisal where available; label unknown details and promotional claims.
```

## 11. Compare two albums

```text
Compare [album A] and [album B] on songwriting, atmosphere/identity, performance, production, and context. Verify original dates and editions. Use cited analysis and my feedback without claiming direct listening. Explain which offers the more useful next contrast for me.
```

## 12. Add my listening feedback

```text
I listened to [artist/album/edition]; [full album/sample]; my reaction was [feedback]; optional date/score [details]. Preserve my words in the journal, leave missing fields blank, and recommend two close matches plus one contrasting experiment. Ask one useful follow-up question.
```

## 13. Listening sequence

```text
Plan a [length]-track or [number]-album exploration sequence for [style/mood/era]. Verify artist/title/album matches and links; explain the progression. Clearly label search links. Provide a plan without claiming playback or a saved playlist.
```

## 14. Death Metal versus Black Metal

```text
Explain musical differences and overlaps between Death Metal and Black Metal using verified contrasting albums and labeled hybrids. Discuss riffs, vocals, rhythm, production, and composition without reducing genres to lyrical stereotypes. Keep this project’s preferences separate from my Death Metal project.
```

## 15. Continue and refresh sources

```text
Summarize verified research, my explicit reactions/decisions, editorial recommendations, and unresolved gaps. Prepare named Markdown updates for the relevant Obsidian notes and identify upload snapshots that need replacing. Do not claim automatic synchronization, saves, or uploads.
```
