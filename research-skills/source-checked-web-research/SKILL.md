---
name: source-checked-web-research
description: Research a topic across websites and produce a source-grounded briefing or evidence base for an essay. Use for substantive web research and source comparisons, rather than simple factual lookups.
---

Build an evidence base that answers the user's actual question. Use the available web search and page-reading tools; this skill adds a research workflow, not new tool access.

- Frame the main question and the decisions the research should support. Infer audience and scope from the conversation; ask only when missing information would materially change the work.
- Search across the relevant subquestions. Prefer sources with direct responsibility or expertise: official agencies, transport operators, tourism boards, original studies, and service providers for their own terms. Use independent reporting or firsthand accounts for perspectives these sources cannot establish, and label those appropriately.
- Open the pages underlying search results before relying on their claims. Record the supporting URL, publisher, relevant publication/update date when available, date checked, claim supported, and material limitations. Keep this working record compact; save a source index when the requested artifact benefits from one.
- Cross-check disputed claims and facts that drive significant decisions against an independent or more authoritative source. Do not count syndicated copies as independent corroboration. Resolve conflicts using date, jurisdiction, method, and direct authority; retain uncertainty when unresolved.
- Distinguish observed facts, source recommendations, personal judgments, and forecasts. For travel, separate seasonal patterns from exact-year forecasts and listing prices from exact-date quotes. Check destination, route, language, timing, inclusions, pricing unit, and currency when relevant.
- Use a source set proportionate to the topic; avoid fixed source quotas and collecting links that add no evidence. Stop when the important questions have adequate support, or explain the remaining evidence gap.
- Deliver an organized synthesis with citations next to the claims they support. Include the research date for changing information. Never fabricate quotations, access dates, source content, or a successful tool call. If a page cannot be read, find an accessible authoritative substitute or identify the limitation.

For an existing travel guide, read its latest relevant notes before interpreting findings. Preserve provisional plans and user preferences. Research does not authorize bookings or external publication.
