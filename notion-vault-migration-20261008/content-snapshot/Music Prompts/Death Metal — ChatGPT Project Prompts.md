## Research add-on

```text
Check online as of today. Open relevant sources and cite them beside facts. Verify original release year, format, artist/album identity, and released versus announced status. Attribute criticism and rankings. State uncertainty and scope. Do not invent listening, scores, quotes, metadata, links, or a consensus. Use my latest feedback and keep project uploads distinct from live Obsidian notes.
```

## 1. Start the project

```text
Read the uploaded project sources. Summarize the learning map, classic starting points, ranking method, current-year release status, and research gaps. Ask which three bands or albums I already like; if I do not know any, suggest three contrasting entry albums. Do not invent my listening history.
```

## 2. Tell the whole history in chapters

```text
Create a chronological Death Metal history from precursors through the current year. Cover demos and tape trading, regional scenes, production, labels, major branching styles, and modern distribution. Separate disputed claims from documented events. Include Latin America and Chile. Begin with a chapter outline, then write a connected sourced overview; offer deeper chapters without pretending an outline is exhaustive.
```

## 3. Explore the subgenre map

```text
Explain the main Death Metal branches, regional sounds, hybrids, and disputed descriptive tags. For each, give short listening cues, two representative original albums with verified years, and one comparison that helps me hear the difference. Include adjacent genres with clear labels. Cover the map broadly without inventing a fixed universal list of all subgenres.
```

## 4. Deep dive into one style

```text
Teach me [style] through its development, characteristic sounds, major scenes, and five representative albums from different periods. Explain overlap and disputed boundaries. Give official listening links and an accessible order. Compare it with the nearest neighboring style.
```

## 5. Classic album listening course

```text
Build a twelve-album learning course from the uploaded classics and any better-supported alternatives. Explain original year, role in the history, listening cues, and how each contrasts with the previous record. Recommend three albums for this week without assuming I have a fixed schedule or already heard them.
```

## 6. Best albums for a year

```text
Research [year] and make a justified top ten Death Metal studio-album ranking, or fewer if the eligible pool is thin. State scope, candidate pool, metadata evidence, rubric, independent critical sources, borderline exclusions, and overlooked alternatives. Keep original years separate from reissues. Show editorial placement separately from published lists and my personal ratings; leave unreported personal ratings blank.
```

## 7. Build the annual archive

```text
Continue the annual archive with [year range, preferably three years at a time]. Research each year individually, create a sourced top five with reasons and overlooked albums, and update coverage status. Do not copy one magazine list as consensus. Keep thin years shorter and identify remaining gaps. Finish with an Obsidian-ready update for the rankings note.
```

## 8. Current-year releases

```text
Search Death Metal releases from the actual current calendar year through today. State year and cutoff date. Verify original album date and released status from artist/label sources. Include varied branches, scenes, and independent labels. Give ten released full-length candidates with listening links; keep future albums, EPs, demos, and reissues in separate sections. Flag changes since the uploaded snapshot.
```

## 9. Best albums so far

```text
Build a current-year best-so-far editorial ranking from a reasonably broad verified released pool. State cutoff, scope, criteria, and limitations; support critical assessment with independent reviews. Keep upcoming albums out and distinguish recommendations from my listening scores. Explain five close placements and identify which three albums I should try next.
```

## 10. Discover emerging bands

```text
Find ten debut or early-discography Death Metal bands worth investigating. Define new, verify formation/debut information where possible, distinguish new projects by experienced musicians, and cover different scenes/styles. Give an entry album or EP, format/date, sonic context, independent evidence where available, and official links. Keep established bands new to me in a separate section. Do not rank an unreleased debut as a heard full album.
```

## 11. Explore Chile and Latin America

```text
Research the development of Death Metal in Chile and its connections to other Latin American scenes. Include early demos, regional distinctions, labels, classic albums, and emerging acts with verified metadata. Explain death/thrash and black/death boundaries where relevant. Avoid making Brazil stand in for the entire region. Provide an original-album listening path and dated sources.
```

## 12. Compare two albums

```text
Compare [album A] and [album B] on riffs, composition, vocals, rhythm, atmosphere, production, and historical context. Verify exact editions and original years. Use cited criticism and my reactions; do not claim direct listening if audio access is unavailable. Explain which is the better next listen for me without calling the result objectively best.
```

## 13. Record my listening and recommend next steps

```text
I listened to [artist / album / edition]. My reaction: [what I liked, disliked, favorite moments, full listen or sample, optional score]. Preserve this feedback in an Obsidian-ready journal entry. Ask one clarifying question if useful, recommend two close matches and one contrasting experiment, and explain the connection. Do not infer listens or scores for other albums.
```

## 14. Make a listening playlist plan

```text
Create a track-list plan that introduces [style / period / scene] in a coherent order. Verify track titles, artist/album matches, and streaming links where possible. Label searches as searches and unavailable items honestly. Explain each selection without quoting full lyrics. Prepare the plan for review; do not save a playlist unless I explicitly ask for creation.
```

## 15. Update the vault and sources

```text
Summarize the verified research and my explicit listening feedback from this chat. Produce concise Markdown updates naming the target Death Metal note and section. Preserve original years, edition details, citations, blank personal fields, and existing links. Separate facts, editorial rankings, published opinions, and my preferences. Explain which project source snapshot should be refreshed; do not claim automatic sync.
```
