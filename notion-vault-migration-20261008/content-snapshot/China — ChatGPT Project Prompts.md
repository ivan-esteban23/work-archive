Copy one prompt into a chat inside your China ChatGPT project. Replace text in square brackets when relevant. You can leave a placeholder unspecified and ask ChatGPT to help decide it. Use the same chat for follow-ups on one decision; start another for a different planning topic.

The user reports creating the ChatGPT project. Its uploaded sources are snapshots; the project does not automatically refresh these Obsidian notes. These prompts build on the project instructions already prepared. **Current means checked when you run a research prompt; it does not select travel dates.** Routes, costs, and activities remain provisional until you choose them, and a choice does not establish a booking.

## Add this to any research prompt

```text
Research online as of today. State the research date, open relevant official or provider pages, and put source links beside material claims. Flag outdated or conflicting facts in my uploaded files. Distinguish confirmed decisions, recommendations, estimates, search prices, exact-date quotes, and confirmed bookings. If browsing or a source is unavailable, identify the specific uncertainty and continue useful planning.

Use my latest confirmed planning state. Do not import Japan/Korea budgets or assume their traveler count applies to China. Keep dates and travel year open unless I have chosen them. Ask only for missing information that affects this task. Do not book, pay, cancel, or contact businesses.
```

## 2. Choose the route

```text
Compare these illustrative 14-night routes against my photography, food, cafés, tea, and browsing interests:
1. Beijing 5 + Xi’an 3 + Shanghai 6.
2. Beijing 5 + Xi’an 3 + Hangzhou 2 + Shanghai 4.
3. Beijing 4 + Chengdu 4 + Shanghai 6.

Show the main experiences, hotel bases, internal transfer days, what each route gives up, and how much unstructured time remains. Explain whether Hangzhou is worth the added transfer. Recommend one route and explain your reasoning without treating it as selected. If my latest priorities favor Chongqing or Zhangjiajie, compare that as an alternative replacing a base rather than adding it to every route. Check practical connections before calling any route feasible.
```

## 3. Compare timing and seasons

```text
For the current candidate route, compare an April–May window with a later-October window and explain whether another window better fits my priorities. Keep the travel year undecided unless I have confirmed it.

Compare climate patterns city by city, photography conditions, tea experiences where relevant, crowds, and practical tradeoffs. Distinguish seasonal patterns from weather forecasts. Check the actual year's published holiday calendar when available; do not extrapolate old holiday dates. Recommend two timing windows to investigate and identify what still depends on the year. Recheck entry eligibility separately for the intended arrival date.
```

## 4. Build a comfortable itinerary

```text
Turn my chosen route into a day-by-day draft. If no route is chosen, use the Beijing 5 / Xi’an 3 / Shanghai 6 proposal and label it clearly.

Show overnight city, morning, afternoon, evening, transfer blocks, and optional alternatives. Group places geographically. Protect meals, rest, photography, and browsing; avoid filling every hour. Day 1 means arrival in China, not departure from Chile. Verify night counts and leave the departure day appropriate to the actual flight when known. Mark ticket reservations, closure days, and any unverified travel time. Do not invent flight or train schedules.
```

## 5. Food, cafés, and tea

```text
Build a geographically useful food, café, and tea shortlist for [city or neighborhood]. Start with relevant candidates in 03_Places_and_Route_Ideas.txt, then check current operating evidence. Confirm dietary preferences if they affect the choices.

Recommend at most six strong places. For each, give the experience or dish, why it fits me, Chinese name, verified branch/address when available, nearest planned activity, booking or queue considerations, price basis if evidenced, Amap search/share link, and dated sources. Compare a food walk, independent meals, or a cooking class when useful. Label unverified hours, prices, and menu items instead of filling gaps.
```

## 6. Vintage, vinyl, books, and gifts

```text

Verify the specific branch and current operating evidence. Give Chinese name, address where verified, what makes the shop relevant, nearby cafés or sights, Amap link, and sources. Separate shops with good evidence from leads needing another check. Do not treat a generic shopping district as proof of a particular store. Suggest meaningful gift ideas within my confirmed shopping allowance, or leave costs open if no allowance is set.
```

## 7. Photography and filming plan

```text
Create a photography and filming plan for [city or the chosen route], using existing candidates such as Jingshan, the Bund, art districts, garden views, and neighborhood streets where relevant.

Suggest a few distinct subjects and geographically sensible sessions, with indoor or poor-weather alternatives. Check current entry, photography, filming, tripod, and equipment rules for restricted venues. Distinguish public viewpoints from paid or private access. Do not guarantee lighting schedules, sunsets, clear views, or permission to film. If dates are known, verify sunrise/sunset times for those dates. Help me leave enough time for photographs without making every day a shoot.
```

```text

Compare the full travel day, likely walking demands, scenery, food/tea appeal, ticket preparation, and whether an overnight stay is worthwhile. For Suzhou, begin with one garden plus Pingjiang Road. For Hangzhou, compare a day visit with the existing two-night tea scenario. Verify actual stations and connections when dates permit; separate train time from door-to-door time. Recommend one excursion or explain why protecting Shanghai time would be better.
```

## 12. Choose hotel areas and properties

```text
Help me choose a base in [city] using the activities in my current itinerary. Compare neighborhoods first for walking, cafés/food, quiet, transport, and airport/station access.

Research specific hotels only after check-in/out dates, traveler count, rooms, and spending scope are known. Compare at most four properties using full-stay totals, taxes/fees, room and bed type, cancellation deadline/time zone, breakfast, and location. Separate property stars from guest ratings and state the review source. Confirm relevant foreign-guest registration procedures with current evidence. Explain the best fit for my trip; do not turn minimum nightly prices into a full-stay quote.
```

```text

Show calculations and label allowances, estimates, search amounts, and dated quotes. Preserve original provider currency and pricing units; cite any exchange rate with its date. Avoid double-counting deposits or paid portions. Identify the largest uncertainties and suggest concrete savings that protect my priorities.
