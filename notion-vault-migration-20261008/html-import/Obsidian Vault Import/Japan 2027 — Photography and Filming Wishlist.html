Chidorigafuchi

Slot: 27 March; a Tokyo buffer can replace it. - [ ] Photo: blossom branches framing the moat; try one reflection and one close detail. - [ ] Film: a steady wide view, water movement, then a blossom close-up; 5–10 seconds per clip. Light: soft morning light or overcast for details; reflections depend on wind. Keep evening footage optional until illumination is confirmed.

Shinjuku Gyoen

Slot: 28 March. - [ ] Photo: garden-wide context, then a branch against a clean background. - [ ] Film: a stationary garden view and blossom movement from a permitted spot. Light: morning within verified access hours; gentle backlight if available. A handheld session keeps the kit simple; see the venue rules below.

Nakameguro

Slot: 29 March. - [ ] Photo: river perspective, reflections, and neighborhood textures. - [ ] Film: one river-wide clip and a nearby detail; record only from a place where stopping is allowed. Light: late afternoon; optional blue-hour/night footage only if access and seasonal lighting are confirmed. Move off busy bridges before composing.

Philosopher’s Path

Slot: 1 April or a swapped Kyoto day. - [ ] Photo: canal leading lines; add petals on water only if conditions provide them. - [ ] Film: steady water, a tree detail, and a short personal reaction from a clear standing spot. Light: soft morning light; overcast can suit blossom detail. Protect the relaxed 2–3 hour photo block.

Historic Kyoto

Slot: 2 April. - [ ] Photo: an allowed Kiyomizu-dera architectural composition, then one historic-street detail. - [ ] Film: a wide view and a texture/detail without blocking steps or foot traffic. Light: morning within visiting hours; choose architecture and textures if blossoms disappoint. Gion footage depends on local permissions and signs.

Fushimi Inari

Slot: 3 April. - [ ] Photo: torii repetition or a close architectural detail from an allowed public position. - [ ] Film: one stationary corridor view and one detail; skip a shot if it would interrupt worship or obstruct movement. Light: try gentler morning light; the full mountain route is optional. No empty-corridor promise.

Asakusa

Slot: 26 March. - [ ] Photo: temple/streetscape context and one small detail; Sumida Park is an optional existing extension. - [ ] Film: a street-wide clip and a purchased snack close-up where permitted. Light: easy morning session; shorten it for jet lag. Do not add a separate outing just for footage.

Matcha and shops

Slots: optional Uji on 4 April, existing café stops, or 7 April shopping. - [ ] Photo: matcha color beside natural window light; souvenir and hands; one personally meaningful record or vintage find. - [ ] Film: tea preparation only if staff agree, cup-to-table detail, and a tasting reaction. For shops, choose an approved detail or record the purchase back at the hotel. Light: natural window light when available; protect the experience and meal time. Uji remains one excursion choice, replacing Nara/Osaka.

Tokyo buffer

Slots: 30 March and 7 April. - [ ] Revisit one missed blossom shot or make a small neighborhood story in the single area chosen that day. Light: choose from current weather and bloom reports. A skyline shot stays optional; visibility and access decide.

Kyoto buffer

Slot: 5 April. - [ ] Revisit the strongest blossom scene; if choosing Arashiyama instead, frame bamboo from the permitted path and capture one steady clip. Light: soft light when available; return with time to pack. The alternative replaces the blossom outing.

Travel moments

Slots: arrival, the two intercity transfers, and departure. - [ ] Packing detail, a window landscape if practical, and a short arrival/return voice note. Avoid showing passport, boarding-pass codes, reservation numbers, or private screens. Filming is optional; transfers and recovery take priority.

A simple film sequence

  1. Opening: a blossom scene or first-impression voice note.
  2. Tokyo: one wide street/river view and one detail.
  3. Journey: a brief window view or packing transition.
  4. Kyoto: canal or temple context, then a detail.
  5. Tea and a personal moment: matcha and one sentence about the experience.
  6. Ending: favorite scene and a closing thought.

For each chosen scene, aim for a wide view → medium view → detail, holding clips about 5–10 seconds with a little stillness before/after the action. Capture a short clean ambient sound where recording is welcome. Use vertical framing for your primary social clips; take a separate horizontal view only if you want a landscape version. Keep recording choices consistent with a kit tested before departure.

Livestreaming is a separate optional activity: choose a permitted location, test the connection/audio, and set a short time limit. Recording footage does not establish permission to broadcast it.

Permissions and venue checks

Shinjuku Gyoen: the official page distinguishes personal non-commercial filming from commercial work, restricts obstructive professional equipment for specified photo activities during busy seasons, and says monetized livestreaming is not permitted. Check the exact activity and equipment rules before visiting. Its current 2027 restriction listing pairs dates with inconsistent weekdays, so reconfirm the dated notice rather than treating it as a final calendar. Official photography/filming rules

Kyoto/Gion: respect designated photography areas, private property, and street access. Do not follow or photograph geiko/maiko without permission or block streets. Kyoto City visitor guidance

For each café, shop, temple, or other venue, check its own rules; asking about photos does not establish permission for video, livestreaming, commercial work, or a tripod. Until confirmed, mark permission as not checked. Sources checked 7 October 2026; recheck before travel.
