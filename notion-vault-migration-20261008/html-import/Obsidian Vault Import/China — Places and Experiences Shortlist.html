The municipal government describes Yangmeizhu Xiejie as an old hutong with cultural and commercial uses and 798 as an art/design district with creative retail and cafés. Those two are particularly good additions for your browsing and photography interests. Beijing tourism/leisure zones, 4 September 2025.

Maliandao is listed by Beijing’s tourism portal as a tea-shopping area on Maliandao Road, Xicheng. Its article dates from 2017: use it to identify the district, not to assume historic shop counts or a particular business is still operating. Visit Beijing — Maliandao. I would make it optional unless tea becomes a major interest; a market visit is not automatically a hosted tasting.

The Palace Museum’s own page says it normally opens Tuesday–Sunday, with national-holiday exceptions, and does not sell same-day tickets. Foreign online reservations require passport numbers. The listed standard admission is CNY 60/person for 1 April–31 October and CNY 40/person for 1 November–31 March, with some galleries extra. These are published tariffs checked today, not dated availability. Official visitor page.

Mutianyu remains the researched Great Wall candidate from the main guide. Official overview.

Xi’an’s municipal overview identifies the wall as a place to walk or cycle with city views and the Muslim Quarter as a collection of food streets. It highlights lamb soup with bread and roujiamo. Treat the quarter as a neighborhood to explore rather than assuming one tourist street contains every worthwhile meal. Xi’an municipal landmark guide.

My food priorities would be yangrou paomo (羊肉泡馍), roujiamo (肉夹馍), and a noodle meal. Confirm the meat/filling at the selected shop; versions vary, and a dish name does not establish dietary suitability. The bread-and-lamb-soup and meat-sandwich descriptions are supported by the municipal guide. Exact restaurants and protein quantities remain unresearched.

Keep the wall visit and food exploration flexible around the reserved museum day. Terracotta Warriors visitor instructions remain the source for ticket/ID checks; the ambiguous filming wording is still unresolved.

Make it personal without filling every day

My suggested five priorities are: one Great Wall outing, one major historic museum, a Xi’an food evening, one art-district outing, and repeat Shanghai riverfront photography. You have not selected these yet. Tea browsing and a café block can replace shopping or another attraction if they appeal more.

For photography, choose one establishing image, one detail, and a short ambient clip per outing where permitted. Old lanes offer doors, textures, and signs; art districts offer industrial forms; the wall and riverfront offer wider compositions. Those are creative suggestions, not permission to film people or inside galleries. Ask staff before using substantial equipment.

Before accepting a tasting or guided experience, establish the price, language, duration, inclusions, and whether buying products is optional. For tea/gifts, record package weight and total, keep receipts, and check Chile’s current import rules before bringing food or plant products home. No import eligibility is asserted here.

A relaxed day can have one fixed attraction, one nearby meal, and a flexible walk or café. Keep M50, 798, and Maliandao as separate location decisions until actual transit is checked. If the trip is solo, verify small portions/half-duck options rather than budgeting a group-sized meal as one-person dining.
