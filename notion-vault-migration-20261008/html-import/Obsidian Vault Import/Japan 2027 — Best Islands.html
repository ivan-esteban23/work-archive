My shortlist for you

Start with Miyajima for the strongest mix of shrine scenery, a possible hike, food, and a manageable western-Japan detour. Choose Naoshima instead if art and architecture are the main reason to visit. Yakushima deserves a larger hiking-focused route, and Ishigaki with Taketomi works as a dedicated beach-and-village stay. Enoshima is the simplest island outing to compare from Tokyo.

Japan's four main islands are major travel regions; this chapter concentrates on smaller island outings rather than treating all of Honshu or Hokkaido as a single stop.

1. Miyajima — my first choice for this guide

Explore the Itsukushima Shrine waterfront, the town, and a selected temple/park walk. Add Mount Misen only if your chosen trail, effort, daylight, and weather suit; a ropeway option still requires checking how much walking remains. In November, Momijidani Park is the foliage lead, with actual color timing checked locally. Miyajima Tourist Association · Official Momijidani guide.

Photo idea: compare the waterfront at different tide stages; look up the actual tide times rather than assume one arrival gives both views. Choose shrine/waterfront photography OR a longer Misen outing as the main block. For food, oysters are a regional lead if you enjoy them; decide from the actual menu. JNTO oyster information.

Access: plan mainland rail to the Hiroshima area, then local travel to Miyajimaguchi and a ferry. A Hiroshima base simplifies luggage and offers a full island day; staying on Miyajima is an alternative if quieter early/late photography is worth the hotel choice. Official ferry operator. Exact dated services and any admission/visitor charges remain unquoted.

2. Naoshima — art and architecture

Choose a small set of art sites rather than race through every museum: Chichu Art Museum, Benesse House Museum, and a Honmura/Art House Project block are starting leads. Build the day around ticket availability, then fit coastal walking and lunch around those slots. Chichu and some other facilities currently use dated/timed admission. Official tickets and facilities.

Access: compare Uno Port, reached via the Okayama area, with Takamatsu Port. Decide your accommodation base before matching ferry arrivals to museum tickets. The official access page contains mixed-year notices, so check the linked operator's actual travel-date timetable. Benesse access.

Photo idea: coastline, architectural lines, and permitted outdoor art. Check each facility's photography/filming rules; do not build your film around unrestricted museum interiors. Teshima is a possible second island day only if you replace other activities and verify its museum slot and ferries. No art festival has been assumed for 2027.

3. Shodoshima — slower scenery and food culture

My two-day split is Olive Park / food-culture browsing on one day and Kankakei Gorge on another. Add Angel Road only when the tide and local access information support the visit; it is a tidal sandbar, not a guaranteed all-day walkway. The island is known for olives, soy sauce, and somen, with gorge greenery and autumn foliage offering different photo subjects. JNTO Shodoshima guide.

Gift idea: a small olive-based product or packaged local food, chosen within the existing shopping cap and checked for your luggage/import needs. Access planning: identify the exact ferry port, accommodation, and onward local bus/taxi/rental plan before booking. Widely separated stops should not be treated as one compact walking route. Local tourism association.

Photo idea: sea and grove textures in May; gorge color in November when reports support it. Prefer this over Naoshima when scenery and food culture matter more than museums.

4. Yakushima — the hiking-focused choice

Start by comparing a Shiratani Unsuikyo forest route with the shorter walking options at Yakusugi Land. Select an actual mapped course by effort; these names cover different lengths and terrain. A Jomon Sugi outing is a separate strenuous planning decision, not a casual extra after a forest stroll. Town trail access information · Official hiking guidance.

Photo idea: moss detail, cedar scale, flowing water, and forest sound. Rain protection for you and the camera matters; check the actual trail/weather notices, particularly for higher routes. A wet island forecast is not a reason to assume every forest trail remains suitable.

Access: investigate air connections and sea access via Kagoshima for the chosen dates; match the actual arrival point to your accommodation and trail transport. JNTO island-access overview. I would reserve 3–4 island nights plus mainland/connection buffer as a starting comparison. Rebuild the 14-night route around it instead of stacking it onto Fuji, Kyoto, Osaka, and another island module.

5–6. Ishigaki and Taketomi — beach base plus village day

Ishigaki: compare staying near the southern town/port for meals and ferry access with a quieter coastal base that needs a more deliberate transport plan. Starting leads are Kabira Bay scenery, one coastal viewpoint, and a guided marine outing suited to your experience. Swimming/snorkeling from Kabira Bay's beach is not permitted; sightseeing there and an operator-approved marine trip elsewhere are different activities. Official Ishigaki guide.

Taketomi: use a separate ferry day for the red-tiled village, streets, crafts, and shore views. The island's minsah weaving is a gift-browsing lead; inspect the actual product and price. Respect residential areas when filming. Official Taketomi guide.

My four-night starting outline: arrival/orientation → Ishigaki coastal day → Taketomi village day → guided marine outing OR weather-flexible land day → departure. Swap the middle days when operators and conditions justify it. This includes only one neighboring island, not a promise that every ferry excursion runs.

Access and season: compare actual flights to Ishigaki, local airport/port travel, and dated ferry services. Warm southern-island scenery does not guarantee beach conditions in May or November. Check wind, waves, and operator advice before choosing water activities. Weather can interrupt flight/ferry plans; leave a mainland buffer before an international departure. Official Okinawa sea guidance.

7. Miyako Island — compare if beaches are the priority

Miyako is my alternative to Ishigaki when the trip's purpose is beach scenery and a relaxed coastal stay. Nearby islands connected by bridges add different viewpoints without making each outing a ferry day. Ishigaki is my first comparison if the Taketomi village visit and ferry-based exploration matter more. Official Miyako Islands guide · JNTO Miyako overview.

Plan: choose one coastal area per day and compare rental car, taxi, bus, or a tour against your actual accommodation location. No car or driving eligibility has been assumed. Follow current beach notices and operator advice for swimming. Photo idea: a wide water-color scene, coastal detail, and a permitted viewpoint; do not stop on a bridge roadway for a shot. Give this 3–4 nights before deciding whether the travel cost is worthwhile.

8. Enoshima — simplest from Tokyo

Use a Tokyo outing for the shrine area, coast, and Sea Candle viewpoint if operating. Choose a manageable island walk with breaks; stairs and an optional cave visit can change the effort. A full Kamakura sightseeing circuit plus every Enoshima stop would make a different, busier day. Official Fujisawa access and attractions.

Photo idea: coastal layers and later light when weather and your return plan suit. A Fuji view is a bonus, not a guarantee or a replacement for your planned Fuji-lakes visit. Check official night-event notices before relying on an illumination; current events are not confirmation for 2027.

Route fit: replace one Tokyo outdoor block, such as the optional Takao day, and return to the same hotel. This is the easiest first island choice when you want to preserve all 14 nights and the Kansai plan.

9. Sado — save for a northern route

Explore Shukunegi's historic port streets, coastal scenery, and selected rice-terrace or cultural stops. Taiko culture is another reason to investigate it; a future festival date or performance requires its own confirmation. JNTO Sado guide.

Access: compare travel to Niigata and the actual ferry route, then establish local transport. A car can help with dispersed sights, but has not been assumed; check a workable bus/taxi/tour alternative if you are not driving. Suggested time is 2–3 island nights plus connections. For your present Tokyo–Fuji–Kansai focus I would put Miyajima or Naoshima ahead of Sado. Sado tourism.
