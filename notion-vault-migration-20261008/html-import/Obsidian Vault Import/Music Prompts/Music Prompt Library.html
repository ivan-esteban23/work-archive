Research add-on

Check online as of today and state the research date. Verify album identity, original release year, edition, and format against responsible artist/label or discography sources. Use attributed independent reviews for critical opinion. Open sources before relying on them. Distinguish fact, classification, recommendation, published ranking, and my personal reaction. Do not invent listening, scores, dates, links, or consensus. Keep uploaded snapshots separate from live notes.

1. Establish the project

I want to explore [genre, artist, scene, era, or musical topic]. Read the project sources if available. Help me understand the history, major styles, essential recordings, current releases, and underexplored artists relevant to this scope.

Ask what I already enjoy without assuming I am a beginner or expert. Offer a chronological path and a sound-based path, then recommend three contrasting starting albums. Keep this project's preferences and listening history separate from my other music projects.

2. History and context

Explain the history of [topic] in connected chapters, from relevant precursors to today. Cover musical changes, regional scenes, recording/production, distribution, cultural context, and key artists. Cite responsible sources and compare disputed origin accounts. Begin with a useful overview and a listening path; do not represent a short outline as a complete history.

3. Styles and neighboring genres

Map the major styles, regional sounds, hybrids, and descriptive tags associated with [topic]. Explain practical listening cues, two representative albums where appropriate, and what separates nearby sounds. Verify album years and acknowledge contested boundaries or artists whose styles change between records. Avoid claiming a fixed universal list of all subgenres.

4. Classics and overlooked records

Recommend essential and overlooked albums for [topic], explaining their musical appeal and historical role separately. Give original release year, exact artist/title, edition considerations, sources, and official listening links or clearly labeled searches. Offer a manageable three-to-five-album starting batch with contrasts rather than an overwhelming list. Keep critical reputation separate from what I personally enjoy.

5. Annual album rankings

Create a sourced, reasoned top [five or ten] album ranking for [topic] in [year]. State eligibility, candidate pool, criteria, and gaps. Verify original releases; keep reissues, live albums, compilations, demos, and EPs separate unless I choose another scope. Use fewer entries when the pool does not support the requested count.

Distinguish your editorial order, named critic/community lists, and my personal favorites. Explain placements and overlooked alternatives. Do not invent numeric scores or treat popularity as musical quality. For the current year, label the list best so far and state the cutoff date.

6. Current releases and announcements

Research [topic] releases from the actual current calendar year through today. Verify full-release status, original dates, and formats from artist/label pages. Include varied scenes and independent releases. Keep released records, future announcements, date changes, singles/EPs, and reissues in separate sections. Do not rank an unavailable full album based on an advance track. Give dated sources, listening links, and three priorities to explore.

7. Emerging artists

Find promising emerging artists in [topic]. Define emerging for this search: newly formed, debut/early-discography, gaining attention, or new to me. Verify career/debut details where available. Distinguish new projects with experienced members from established acts releasing new material. Recommend an entry recording with evidence and explain why it might interest me; label promotional claims and avoid unsupported popularity metrics.

8. Compare recordings

Compare [recording A] with [recording B] on songwriting, performance, sound/production, identity, and context. Verify exact editions and original years. Use cited analysis and my feedback without claiming direct listening that did not occur. Explain the strongest reason to try each and which is the better next step for my current interests.

9. Record feedback and discover more

I listened to [artist / album / edition]. My reaction was [what worked, what did not, favorite moments, full listen or sample, optional score]. Preserve my words in a dated listening-journal entry. Leave missing information blank rather than assuming it.

Recommend two close matches and one contrasting experiment, explaining the connection. Ask one useful preference question if needed. Do not assign listening sessions or scores to other albums.

10. Listening playlist plan

Prepare a listening sequence for [topic or mood], with verified track/artist/album matches and links where possible. Explain the transitions and learning purpose. Label title searches and unavailable items honestly. Provide the plan for review; do not save a playlist, alter my library, or claim playback unless I explicitly request the action and it succeeds.

11. Continue and update my notes

Summarize this project's verified research, my explicit decisions and listening feedback, editorial recommendations, and remaining gaps. Prepare concise Markdown updates naming the target note and section. Preserve original years, edition details, source links, and my wording. Do not claim automatic synchronization between ChatGPT and Obsidian. Tell me which uploaded snapshot should be refreshed after I save the updates.
