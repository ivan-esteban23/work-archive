2. History in chapters

Create a sourced chapter plan for Heavy Metal from precursors through the 1970s, NWOBHM, international scenes, later decades, and traditional revival. Start the first chapter. Compare disputed origins and explain music, labels, production, distribution, and cultural context. Do not present an outline as the whole history.

3. Map styles and neighbors

Explain traditional Heavy Metal, NWOBHM, NWOTHM, epic/melodic approaches, and neighboring speed, doom, power, progressive, hard rock/glam, thrash, death, and black branches. Distinguish style, movement, scene, and descriptor. Give two sourced album examples where supported and label disputed boundaries.

4. Classic albums

Recommend five classic or overlooked albums for [era/style], with original years, edition notes, historical role, musical appeal, and sourced listening links. Include varied artists rather than only Maiden and Priest. Distinguish reputation from my enjoyment.

5. NWOBHM beyond famous names

Research NWOBHM beyond Iron Maiden. Explain the scene and discover lesser-known recordings with verified years, roles, and sources. Distinguish older influences from the new generation and do not assume all British metal is NWOBHM.

6. Modern traditional revival

Explain NWOTHM and recommend contrasting contemporary entry albums with verified dates. Compare revival, influence, and originality through attributed criticism. Keep established acts separate from debut projects.

7. Regional scene

Research Heavy Metal in [country/region, for example Chile, Japan, Germany, or the US]. Develop sourced local history, classic albums, modern artists, and stylistic variety without treating a nation as one sound. Identify remaining gaps.

8. Rank one year

Build a top [five/ten] for [year]. Start with traditional Heavy Metal original studio albums; state candidate pool, hybrid eligibility, original years, criteria, evidence, and omissions. Separate editorial order, published lists, and my favorites. Do not invent scores or pad thin pools.

9. Develop annual archive

Research [year range] one year at a time. Verify candidates before ranking, use original rather than reissue years, and track gaps and confidence. Compare named published lists without inventing consensus. Prepare Markdown updates for the annual archive.

10. Current releases

Search the actual current calendar year through today. State cutoff and scope, verify responsible artist/label pages, and separate released full albums, upcoming announcements, EPs/demos/splits, and reissues. Include smaller acts and varied scenes. Recommend three priorities without ranking an unavailable album.

11. Emerging bands

Find promising debut/early-discography Heavy Metal projects for [year/style/region]. Verify debut and formation details where possible, label experienced-member projects and unknown dates, and give entry albums plus independent appraisal where available. Do not call established acts new because of a new album.

12. Compare albums

Compare [album A] and [album B] on songwriting, identity, performance, production, and context. Verify original years and editions, use cited analysis and my feedback, and do not claim direct listening. Explain the best reason to try each.

13. Record my feedback

14. Listening sequence

Plan a [length]-track or [number]-album sequence for [era/style/mood]. Verify identities, link official recordings or label title searches, and explain the progression. Do not claim playback or save a playlist unless I ask and the action succeeds.

15. Continue and refresh notes

Summarize verified research, editorial recommendations, my explicit feedback/decisions, and remaining gaps. Prepare named Markdown updates for the relevant notes and identify uploaded snapshots to replace. Do not claim automatic Obsidian synchronization, saves, or uploads.
