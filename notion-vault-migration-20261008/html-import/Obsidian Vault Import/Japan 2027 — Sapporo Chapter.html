My starting recommendation

Start with three nights: arrival and a short central evening → Sapporo city day → Otaru day trip → departure. Choose four nights if you also want Jozankei hot springs or a separate Moerenuma photography day. Three nights give two full outing days, not three. Four nights are my preferred summer version if reducing another base feels worthwhile.

Sapporo is a Hokkaido base, substantially separate from your Kansai and Kyushu options. Compare it with Fukuoka as another regional choice; do not automatically add both to the same 14 nights. Keep Fuji as its own scenic/summit decision.

Outdoor views and photography

Mt. Moiwa: start by comparing a ropeway visit, rather than assign a summit hike without knowing your preferred effort. Check the operator's current notices and access map for the streetcar/shuttle/walking connection, operation, tickets, and final descent. A map pin at the lower station is not a complete ascent. Choose a clear forecast; keep dinner/shop plans flexible.

Moerenuma Park: Isamu Noguchi's landscape design, geometric landforms, and the Glass Pyramid make this my strongest optional architecture/photo outing. Reach the park using the operator's described Toho subway to Kanjodori-Higashi → Chuo Bus to the appropriate park entrance route, then verify the dated bus timetable and return. Entrance access and facilities change seasonally. Check the actual fountain schedule before planning a fountain shot; the current English page carries operating restrictions. Park design, transport, and facilities.

Filming sequence: a central street establishing shot → park/architecture detail → permitted food preparation or meal close-up → Otaru waterfront movement → city lights. Ask before recording inside shops, restaurants, shrine interiors, or museums. Moerenuma publishes photography/video requirements on its English page; consult those for a planned shoot. A small personal camera does not establish permission for every use.

Otaru day trip

Route: Sapporo Station → a dated JR service to Otaru Station → walk the central area → JR back to Sapporo. Confirm whether your service is direct or needs a change. Use JR Hokkaido's Sapporo/Otaru service information and the Otaru tourist association for updated visitor information.

My day: canal and warehouse-area photos → seafood lunch → Sakaimachi-area browsing → one café/sweet → return. The canal and Sakaimachi shopping area are separate walking blocks; cut shopping if your pace or weather requires. Otaru's historic harbor/warehouse character is described by Hokkaido's official tourism guide.

Blue-hour canal photos are optional. Check that the actual return train, shop closures, and dinner plan support a later stay. No canal cruise, glass workshop, music-box purchase, or specific café seat has been booked.

Jozankei hot springs — fourth-night choice

Route: central Sapporo → Jotetsu Kappa Liner or another confirmed bus → your selected Jozankei stop → short town/river walk and a selected day-use bath → bus back. Compare the tourist association's access page with the operator's multilingual transport page for the actual boarding point, timetable, booking requirements, and return. Reserve the Kappa Liner when required; do not substitute an old bus-stop screenshot for a confirmed seat.

Choose the bathing facility first: check day-use admission, last entry, closures, towels, and house rules, then coordinate the bus. Use the association's hot-spring listings to compare options. River walks depend on weather/access. Autumn viewpoints and seasonal shuttles need a dated check; the official foliage program is a planning lead, not confirmation of 2027 bus operation or peak colors.

This is a separate outing from Otaru. A Jozankei overnight would replace a Sapporo hotel night and change luggage/transfers; it has not been added. Furano/Biei, Hakodate, and Niseko would need their own wider Hokkaido route rather than extra compulsory day trips in this short chapter.
