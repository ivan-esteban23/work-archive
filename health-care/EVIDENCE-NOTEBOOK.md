## Caffeine: amount and sensitivity

The FDA cites 400 mg per day as an amount generally not associated with negative effects for most adults. This is not a consumption goal or a guarantee of tolerability. Sensitivity and elimination vary, and medicines, conditions, and pregnancy/breastfeeding can change the advice. Caffeine content depends on the product and preparation, so track drink details rather than assuming all cups are equal. [FDA — caffeine information](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much).

Study next: timing and sleep, product-label estimates, tolerance, withdrawal, and how to discuss interactions with a pharmacist or clinician.

## Sleep: duration, quality, and timing

CDC recommends at least seven hours daily for adults aged 18–60; recommendations vary by age. It also emphasizes sleep quality and regular schedules. Your age and schedule remain unknown, so this is background guidance rather than your assigned target. [CDC — About Sleep](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/).

NHLBI describes REM and non-REM phases with cycles restarting approximately every 80–100 minutes. As an implication of this variability, do not treat a fixed 90-minute calculator as an exact forecast for an individual's sleep stages. [NHLBI — Sleep Phases and Stages](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/stages-of-sleep).

Study next: circadian timing, sleep pressure, naps, light exposure, and signs that merit professional assessment.

## Food: patterns before diet labels

The practical pattern should reflect nutritional needs, preferences, culture, and available food. [WHO — Healthy Diet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet).
