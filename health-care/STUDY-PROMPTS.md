# Health Care study prompts

Use one prompt per focused conversation. Ask for current sources whenever a health claim or recommendation is involved.

## Caffeine

1. Teach me how caffeine affects alertness and sleep. Explain adenosine, half-life, sensitivity, and tolerance in plain language. Separate general mechanisms from predictions about me.
2. Help me estimate my caffeine intake from the drinks I actually report. Ask for brand, serving size, preparation, and timing. Prefer product information and use ranges when the amount is uncertain; do not treat a cup as a fixed dose.
3. Explain how caffeine timing might relate to sleep. Ask about my schedule before suggesting a low-risk adjustment. Explain why a daily reference amount is not a target or a guarantee for every person.
4. Compare coffee, tea, matcha, energy drinks, and decaffeinated drinks using equivalent serving sizes where possible. Keep caffeine, added sugar, and other ingredients separate.

## Sleep

5. Teach me the difference between sleep stages, sleep cycles, and circadian rhythm. Explain why a fixed 90-minute alarm calculator cannot determine my ideal wake-up time.
6. Review my seven-day sleep journal. Distinguish sleep opportunity, estimated sleep duration, regularity, awakenings, and daytime functioning. Identify patterns without diagnosing a disorder.
7. Explain naps, evening light, screens, caffeine, and alcohol in relation to sleep. Give me the strength of evidence and practical limitations rather than a rigid routine.
8. Help me understand when persistent sleepiness, insomnia, loud snoring, or breathing pauses warrant a clinical discussion. Ask about symptoms rather than assuming I have them.

## Food and diets

9. Teach me balanced eating, protein, fiber, fats, carbohydrates, and hydration using ordinary foods. Keep it focused on health and everyday function; do not assume a calorie deficit is appropriate.
10. Compare Mediterranean-style eating, vegetarian diets, low-carbohydrate diets, and intermittent fasting. Discuss nutritional adequacy, evidence, adherence, cost, and situations needing professional guidance. Do not select a diet for me before learning my goals and context.
11. Help me read a food label that I provide. Explain serving size, ingredients, fiber, sugar, sodium, and what the label cannot tell us.
12. Help me build a few affordable meal options around my preferences, allergies, cooking access, and schedule. Ask for those details before designing the options.

## Habits and review

13. Help me understand one habit I name: its trigger, the need it meets, the immediate reward, and its longer-term effects. Suggest one achievable replacement without moralizing.
14. Review my journal for patterns connecting food, caffeine, sleep, stress, movement, and energy. Consider alternative explanations and missing data. Suggest one question worth exploring next.
15. Evaluate a health claim or article I provide. Trace the evidence, distinguish human outcomes from animal or laboratory studies, and explain absolute effects when available. Give a confidence level and unresolved questions.
16. Run a short weekly review: what did I learn, what did I observe, what felt sustainable, and what should I change next? Keep conclusions proportional to the data and prepare clinician questions if appropriate.
