## Main correction: the 2025 sitting-break review is misrepresented

Claude says this review found only nonsignificant downward glucose trends. Its main results instead report significant reductions in glucose incremental area under the curve (SMD −0.49, 95% CI −0.85 to −0.14) and insulin incremental area under the curve (SMD −0.26, 95% CI −0.50 to −0.03). Other glucose endpoints were not uniformly significant. Identify the specific outcome rather than using one nonsignificant measure to summarize the entire study.

These remain acute metabolic results in adults with obesity, with substantial heterogeneity for glucose. They do not prove prevention of diabetes or cardiovascular disease or establish a universal break schedule. [Original Frontiers review, published 20 November 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1708301/full); [original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41356824/). Publisher sections were retrieved through indexed excerpts after direct opening failed.

## Other qualifications

- **Mindfulness:** “only against active controls” should describe Goyal's eligibility criteria, not imply mindfulness works exclusively against such comparators. The review found moderate evidence for anxiety/depression/pain, lower evidence for stress/distress, and no demonstrated superiority over active treatments. Lack of demonstrated superiority is not proof of equal effects. [Goyal original review abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24395196/).
- **Breathing trial:** sample size is available: 108 enrolled, with relatively small individual groups. Recruitment was mainly from a Stanford undergraduate class; the trial was remote and retrospectively registered. Its promising mood results should retain those limits. [Original Balban paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/).
- **Standing versus walking:** the 2022 review's walking breaks improved both glucose and insulin compared with uninterrupted sitting; standing's evidence was more limited. Do not let the standing-only summary obscure this distinction. [Original review abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35147898/).
- **Habit stacking:** “not a studied technique” is stronger than the search supports. Say that this review did not verify direct evidence for the popular label; routine-based cues and implementation intentions have related research.
- **Readiness scores:** failure to retrieve validation is a search gap, not proof that no validation or outcome studies exist. Keep that boundary explicit.
- **Everyday movement:** standing can replace sedentary time, but standing quietly is not equivalent to brisk walking or a muscle-strengthening session. [WHO activity guidance](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity).
