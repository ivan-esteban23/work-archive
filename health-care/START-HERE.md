# Health Care — Daily Life & Habits

Created 2026-10-12. A personal learning project about caffeine, food, sleep, movement, stress, and everyday routines.

## Purpose

Understand how daily choices relate to energy, concentration, mood, sleep, and wellbeing. Learn the evidence, observe your own patterns, and choose practical changes that fit your life. Weight loss is not an assumed goal.

## Learning map

| Topic | What we will study | What we can observe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caffeine | Sources, amounts, timing, sensitivity, tolerance, withdrawal | Drink details, approximate dose, time, sleep and symptoms |
| Food and diets | Balanced eating, protein, fiber, fats, labels, evidence behind popular diets | Meal patterns, hunger, satisfaction, practical constraints |
| Sleep | Sleep stages, circadian rhythm, sleep opportunity, naps, regularity | Bed/wake times, estimated sleep, awakenings, daytime sleepiness |
| Habits | Triggers, rewards, environment, replacements, consistency | What happened before and after a behavior |
| Movement and recovery | Activity, sedentary time, rest, gradual progression | Comfortable movement and how it felt |
| Stress and attention | Workload, breaks, screens, relaxation, mood | Stress level, breaks, energy and concentration |

## First month: proposed learning sequence

1. **Week 1 — Learn and observe.** Start with sleep and caffeine basics. Keep a brief journal for seven days without deliberately disrupting your routine. Unknown values stay unknown.
2. **Week 2 — Explore one small change.** Review the journal and choose a manageable habit, such as a consistent wind-down cue. Agree what to observe and what would make you stop. Do not deliberately increase caffeine or restrict sleep to test a theory.
3. **Week 3 — Food and daily rhythm.** Learn balanced eating and evaluate diet claims. Identify one practical improvement without assuming calorie tracking or weight loss is needed.
4. **Week 4 — Review and keep what fits.** Compare observations, explain alternative causes, and decide whether to continue, adjust, or seek professional advice. A personal journal cannot establish a diagnosis or prove causation.

The order can change when you choose a priority. No personal medical facts or completed habit changes have been entered.
