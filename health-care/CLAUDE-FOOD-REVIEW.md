## Corrections and gaps

### 1. Fasting conclusions need current evidence

The broad “does not outperform” wording is too categorical for current evidence. A June 2025 network meta-analysis reported an additional 1.29 kg weight reduction with alternate-day fasting versus continuous restriction, with moderate certainty. The authors judged this below their 2 kg threshold for a minimally important difference. Thus, the evidence supports broadly similar effects with a small protocol-specific difference, not universal superiority or universal equivalence. The review searched through November 2024; it does not establish comprehensive coverage through October 2026. Publisher text was available through indexed excerpts, not a successful direct full-page fetch. [Original BMJ review](https://www.bmj.com/content/389/bmj-2024-082007).

The Lowe time-restricted-eating trial compared with consistent meal timing, not daily calorie restriction. Do not combine those comparators in a single blanket conclusion. Liu's trial tested adding a time window to prescribed calorie restriction: the absence of a significant additional effect does not by itself prove the exact mechanism or exclude every possible timing benefit. [Liu original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35443107/).

### 2. Cochrane details are available from Cochrane itself

The relevant review was published 28 January 2022 and included 61 trials with 6,925 participants. It found probably little to no difference in weight change up to two years and several assessed risk markers. In 45 of 61 trials the energy prescription or restriction approach was similar between groups; that is not proof that actual consumed calories were precisely matched in every trial. Avoid making “when calories are matched” the definition of the entire current evidence base. [Cochrane review](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD013334_low-carbohydrate-diets-or-balanced-carbohydrate-diets-which-works-better-weight-loss-and-heart).

### 3. PREDIMED needs the main result alongside the sensitivity analysis

The report highlights a secondary expert account where the nut arm was nonsignificant after excluding a site. The corrected paper's main adjusted analysis reported significant associations for both intervention arms: hazard ratios 0.69 for olive oil and 0.72 for nuts, with confidence intervals below 1. Explain which analysis a statement refers to; a sensitivity result should not stand in for the main result. These are high-risk older adults in Spain, not direct evidence for every population. [Corrected original paper](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1800389).

### 4. The late-dinner trial is from 2020, not 2024

The 20-person comparison of 22:00 and 18:00 dinners is Gu and colleagues' 2020 JCEM study. It assessed acute metabolic responses with a fixed sleep period, not long-term weight gain or a universal meal cutoff. Correct the year and replace indirect commentary with the original study. [Original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32525525/).

### 5. Basic education is underdeveloped

The nutrition section barely explains protein, carbohydrates, fiber, fats or nutritional adequacy. It says dependable food-group guidance was not retrieved, despite current official guidance being available. WHO's January 2026 fact sheet covers these foundations and food sources. Guidelines are appropriate for introductory education; each food group does not need its own isolated trial before it can be explained. [WHO healthy diet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet).

Hydration, hunger, concentration and reflux remain largely unresearched. Do not equate failed retrieval with absence of evidence.

### 6. Label instructions depend on country and product

“Labels list sodium, not salt” and “labels report nutrients per serving” are too universal. Labels may use sodium or salt and may show per-100-g/ml as well as serving information. The revised explanation should identify the label jurisdiction, then scale the displayed basis to the amount eaten. The user's country-specific label system was not audited here.
