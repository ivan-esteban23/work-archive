## Remaining corrections

### Drake's primary results remain unnecessarily unresolved

The original discussion contains the 17:00 interpretation. It also reports an objective sleep-duration loss exceeding an hour at six hours before bedtime. The diary estimate was 41 minutes and did not reach significance (p = .08). These are different measures, not a reason to average publicity and commentary estimates. “Participants generally did not notice” should be restricted to the six-hour objective/diary discrepancy.

The table also describes one active pill and two placebos “per dose time.” The protocol was three pills per study day, one at each time, with one active pill on a caffeine day; the placebo day had three placebos. Nonstudy caffeine was allowed before 16:00 and reported as similar across conditions. [Original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3805807/).

The new actigraphy label is not adequately supported. Retain the original paper's description of a validated portable sleep monitor rather than replacing it with a measurement category inferred from commentary. Zeo headband technology uses electrophysiological signals; it should not be conflated with movement-based actigraphy. [Original device validation study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11325-012-0757-4).

### Coverage is added, but introductory explanations remain thin

Sleep stages are described mostly through caffeine's effects on N3 and light sleep. The report still needs a plain explanation of N1, N2, N3 and REM, and their changing distribution across the night. Use [NHLBI's introductory guide](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/stages-of-sleep). Beverage amounts remain prose instead of a comparison table; decaf/matcha source gaps remain disclosed rather than resolved.

EFSA's own current official summary explicitly includes possible sleep effects from 100 mg near bedtime. Claude should distinguish uncertainty about retrieving the full opinion passage from absence of authoritative guidance. [EFSA summary](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine).

### Bibliographic details can be completed

The cycle study is in Sleep Health, volume 10, issue 1 supplement, February 2024, S52–S62 (DOI dated 2023). Its 369-person sample and 96-minute median are supported. [Original journal record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352721823002048).

Schöllhorn's display-light paper is Communications Biology 6, 228, published 1 March 2023. [Publisher record](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-023-04598-4). Gardiner's journal issue is April 2025, with online publication October 2024. Keep the dates separate.

Different trial timings do not categorically make pooling impossible. They require a suitable synthesis method rather than a naive average. Also, conflicting statements about tolerance to specific sleep effects should not make dependence and withdrawal sound equally unresolved.
