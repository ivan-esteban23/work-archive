## A repeatable research workflow

1. **Define the visual question.** Choose one subject per board: a silhouette, fabric, styling detail, palette or page treatment.
2. **Collect with context.** Save the original page or creator post, image credit, publication date, country/city, and the styling detail you want to study.
3. **Compare references.** Use several independent examples. Keep runway, street photography, product listings, historical objects and AI interpretations visibly separate.
4. **Build the board.** Group by silhouette, material, palette and accessory. Add a sentence explaining each selection instead of collecting lookalikes without context.
5. **Keep the final record in Obsidian.** Store permitted local copies with the source record; revisit the original page before publication. A collection link alone is not a durable backup.

## Rechecked from the earlier shortlist

- **[We Heart It](https://weheartit.com/): keep as a legacy lead, not a recommended starting tool.** Its retrieved public homepage showed support/legal links rather than a usable discovery or board interface. This does not prove that every app or account feature is unavailable.
- **Are.na:** channels can preserve your organization, but external sources can still change or disappear. The earlier “no pin rot” claim was too strong.
- **Dewu and Xiaohongshu:** removed the unsupported “best” and “strongest” rankings. Their usefulness depends on accessible posts, language and source verification.
- **Shaarli:** the official GitHub repository remains the reliable project reference here; the earlier claim about another domain’s TLS certificate was not reverified.
- **Pinterest links:** availability and hotlink behavior vary. Keep provenance and permitted local copies rather than assuming every link will fail.

## Publication and verification notes

Links support discovery and research; they do not grant magazine reproduction rights. Record the individual image’s terms or permission status. Native app access, login flows, subscription features and installations were not tested. No accounts were created and no collection service was connected.

The existing reference-credit convention (`images/references/CREDITS.md`) can be reused if that file exists in the project; no new credit ledger was populated by this directory update.
