## Make it personal without filling every day

My suggested five priorities are: one Great Wall outing, one major historic museum, a Xi’an food evening, one art-district outing, and repeat Shanghai riverfront photography. You have not selected these yet. Tea browsing and a café block can replace shopping or another attraction if they appeal more.

For photography, choose one establishing image, one detail, and a short ambient clip per outing where permitted. Old lanes offer doors, textures, and signs; art districts offer industrial forms; the wall and riverfront offer wider compositions. Those are creative suggestions, not permission to film people or inside galleries. Ask staff before using substantial equipment.

Before accepting a tasting or guided experience, establish the price, language, duration, inclusions, and whether buying products is optional. For tea/gifts, record package weight and total, keep receipts, and check Chile’s current import rules before bringing food or plant products home. No import eligibility is asserted here.

A relaxed day can have one fixed attraction, one nearby meal, and a flexible walk or café. Keep M50, 798, and Maliandao as separate location decisions until actual transit is checked. If the trip is solo, verify small portions/half-duck options rather than budgeting a group-sized meal as one-person dining.

## Excursion or new base?

**Suzhou:** my first excursion candidate from Shanghai for gardens and canal photography. Choose the Humble Administrator’s Garden or research the Lingering Garden as an alternative; UNESCO lists both within the classical-gardens property. One garden, Pingjiang Road, and a meal are a more useful starting plan than buying a package covering several gardens, temples, hills, and a second water town. Compare the actual rail stations, local transfers, admission reservations, and return connection once dates are set. [UNESCO gardens](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/813/) · [Suzhou overview](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-TravelAcrossChina/20250521/2cd76b748ca1433c98044118867371c5.html).

**Zhujiajiao:** another water-town option within Shanghai, with riverside buildings and Fangsheng Bridge. Compare it with Suzhou on the scenery you want and the complete travel day. I would select one initially and keep the other optional. Boat rides and attraction tickets require separate inclusion checks; a town visit does not establish that every site or boat is included. [Shanghai heritage overview](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-HeritageZones/20240827/66903de4b44047a5aeb1284a2148fc09.html).

**Hangzhou:** allow an arrival/lake block, a Longjing tea block, and an onward-transfer block if staying two nights. Keep tea purchasing separate from workshop fees and ask for the quantity and full purchase price. A provider offers picking, hand-frying, and tasting, but future picking conditions, dates, and language availability remain to be checked in China — Klook Experience Research 2026-10-08 [source: 05_Evidence_and_Experience_Research.txt; note: China — Klook Experience Research 2026-10-08].

**Chengdu:** a route replacement for food, lanes, and an optional panda-base visit. The municipal Sichuan feature identifies Jinli, Kuanzhai, and the Chengdu panda base. Begin with one food/neighborhood day and research the panda outing separately; admission does not guarantee close animal encounters. Specific tea houses and restaurants still need verification. [Sichuan overview](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-TravelAcrossChina/20250421/fa4666cf783d46f48fc6edd4cf9dcf95.html).

**Chongqing:** a route replacement if urban night photography and hotpot matter more than Xi’an history. Hongyadong and Liziba are starting points from the official overview, not a finished walking route. Check actual viewpoints, crowd conditions, hills/stairs, river crossings, and hotel access before choosing a base. Do not assume every camera viewpoint is public or every skyline light operates nightly. [Chongqing overview](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-TravelAcrossChina/20250514/0a8f03830bcc493190c6b6025e4898b5.html).

**Zhangjiajie / Wulingyuan:** the strongest landscape alternative in this round. UNESCO describes a landscape of sandstone pillars and ravines. Treat it as a dedicated city/park chapter, with weather flexibility and a checked entrance plan. Any Tianmen Mountain or glass-bridge ticket would require its own location and inclusion research; this Wulingyuan source does not verify those products. [UNESCO Wulingyuan](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/640/).
