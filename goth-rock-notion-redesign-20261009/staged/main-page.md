<summary>Six sound cues</summary>
	### Rhythm and grip
	Follow the pulse, then the bass line. Catchiness might come from a recurring bass figure, a drum accent, or the vocal phrasing. Name the element holding your attention.
	### Attack and weight
	Attack is the initial strike of a note or drum hit. Weight concerns the impression of depth and force. Loud playback, strong bass, distorted guitars, and hard drumming do not always produce the same effect.
	### Guitar texture
	Distinguish sharp rhythmic strokes, ringing melodic lines, abrasive noise, and sustained layers. A heavily effected guitar does not automatically mean a heavier song.
	### Space and atmosphere
	Reverb gives a sense of space and decay. Delay adds repeated echoes. Listen for the balance between close, immediate sounds and distant layers.
	### Voice and character
	Notice register, urgency, theatrical phrasing, and restraint. Sinister atmosphere might come from delivery rather than lyrics or low pitch alone.
	### Hooks and identity
	Identify the moment you remember after the track ends. Then ask what separates the band from others you enjoy: rhythm, tone, melody, arrangement, or vocal character.
</details>
