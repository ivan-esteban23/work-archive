## A band assembled from the punk underground {color="purple"}
45 Grave makes a useful first stop because its history connects deathrock to working punk musicians rather than presenting the style as an isolated costume. Trouser Press traces the group through Dinah Cancer and guitarist Paul B. Cutler, with Don Bolles from the Germs and Rob Graves from the Bags and Gun Club; Paul Roessler brought keyboards into the developing lineup. The same account locates early releases in the 1981 Black Cross/Wax single and Hell Comes to Your House compilation. These are historical connections, not proof that every song sounds like those earlier bands. [Trouser Press — 45 Grave](https://trouserpress.com/reviews/45-grave/).
Liz Ohanesian’s 2009 scene guide places 45 Grave alongside Christian Death and Kommunity FK while emphasizing its playful horror-film approach to punk. This gives the issue a useful opening question: can theatrical darkness make a song more inviting rather than less? [LA Weekly — The LA Deathrock Starter Guide](https://www.laweekly.com/the-la-deathrock-starter-guide/).
## The album: Sleep in Safety {color="purple"}
The original album appeared on Enigma in 1983. Real Gone Music’s reissue page names Cancer, Cutler, Bolles, Graves and Roessler and reproduces an eleven-track, two-sided sequence. Its superlatives about the band are promotional opinion. [Real Gone Music — album and sequence](https://realgonemusic.com/products/45-grave-sleep-in-safety-vinyl).
The magazine’s listening link opens a fourteen-track expanded edition. Its sequence differs from the LP and includes Riboflavin, School’s Out and a separately labeled Partytime single version. Start by identifying which version you have, then treat the additional material as a separate comparison. [Apple Music — linked edition](https://music.apple.com/us/album/sleep-in-safety/162551605).
## Three ways into the record {color="purple"}
Trouser Press praises the album’s variety, contrasting Evil’s catchiness with Surf Bat’s surf instrumental character and the more layered constructions elsewhere. That is the publication’s assessment. The following questions are this magazine’s proposed listening exercise, not findings from a listening session. [Trouser Press](https://trouserpress.com/reviews/45-grave/).
- **Evil:** after one sample, which gesture can you remember: voice, guitar figure, bass movement or drum accent? Write down the element rather than a general mood.
- **Insurance from God:** follow one instrument through the song. Does your attention return to a recurring idea, or does another part take over?
- **Surf Bat:** test whether a change in character strengthens the album for you. Compare its role in the sequence with the vocal tracks around it.
## Read the LP as two sides {color="purple"}
Use this sequence to understand the album’s original architecture; the source is a later label reissue listing, cross-checked against the existing discography research. It does not identify the manufacture date of your own copy. [Real Gone Music](https://realgonemusic.com/products/45-grave-sleep-in-safety-vinyl).
- **Side one:** Insurance from God → Evil → Partytime → Dream Hits II → Slice O’ Life.
- **Side two:** 45 Grave → Phantoms → Violent World → Bad Love → Surf Bat → Procession.
**Listening experiment:** hear each side as a short arc. What changes when Surf Bat is near the end rather than treated as an isolated novelty?
## Versions and the next chapter {color="purple"}
Keep the album and soundtrack incarnations of Partytime separate. The label ties a later version to The Return of the Living Dead; this feature does not settle the differing single-year accounts or assume that every version carries the same lyrics. [Real Gone Music](https://realgonemusic.com/products/45-grave-sleep-in-safety-vinyl).
For a discography branch, Trouser Press lists Autopsy (1987), Only the Good Die Young (1989) and Pick Your Poison (2012). Its account describes Autopsy as archival material and Only the Good Die Young as a live recording. These are different kinds of releases, not three equivalent studio-album successors. [Trouser Press](https://trouserpress.com/reviews/45-grave/).
