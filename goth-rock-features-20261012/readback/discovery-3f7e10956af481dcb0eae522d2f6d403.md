## A band built around a personal vision {color="purple"}
The band’s own history dates Patrik Mata’s formation of Kommunity FK to 1978 and identifies surrealism, Dalí, Jean Cocteau and David Bowie among his inspirations. Read that page as the artist’s account: its claims of uniqueness are self-description, not an independent verdict. [Official history](https://kommunityfk.wixsite.com/kommunityfk/history).
The 1983 debut belongs to a Los Angeles network with room for abrasive punk, experiments and gloom. Greg Fasolino’s Trouser Press review places it between Joy Division, Christian Death and Savage Republic and hears both sparse guitar and prominent bass. These comparisons are critical descriptions, not a verified chain of influence. [Trouser Press — Kommunity FK](https://trouserpress.com/reviews/kommunity-fk/).
## The album as a performance document {color="purple"}
In Liz Ohanesian’s November 2025 Beatique interview, Mata recalls making The Vision and the Voice live in the studio with few overdubs, using the band’s contemporary set as its foundation. This is participant testimony about the method; it does not supply a complete track-by-track session sheet. [Beatique — interview with Patrik Mata](https://www.beatique.net/kommunity-fk-returns-to-l-a-for-independent-project-records-anniversary-shows).
That account suggests a listening question rather than a quality judgment: does the repetition feel like a band building pressure together? Rawness need not mean absence of arrangement. Listen for how a part keeps its identity while the surrounding performance changes.

## Why the running order matters {color="purple"}
The 1983 IP 008 LP listing supplies this order; a 2010 retrospective independently reproduces it. Both are secondary sources, and the retailer imports its data from Discogs. Their agreement supports the route but is not a direct inspection of an original sleeve. [LP listing](https://www.highfidelityla.com/release/9086272/kommunity-fk-the-vision-and-the-voice) · [James Von Sutekh — retrospective](https://plunderthetombs.blogspot.com/2010/12/kommunity-fk-vision-and-voice.html).
- **Side one:** Restrictions → Bullets → Incompatible Disposition → Tribulations → Unknown to You → Fuck the Kommunity.
- **Side two:** To Blame → No Fear → Nothing Yet → Anti-Pop → We Will Not Fall.
The 1994 Cleopatra CD listing begins with Unknown to You and We Will Not Fall. Choosing an edition therefore changes the entrance into the record. [Musik-Sammler — 1994 CD](https://www.musik-sammler.de/release/kommunity-fk-the-vision-and-the-voice-cd-510948/).
## Credits and the synthesizer question {color="purple"}
The LP metadata names Mata on guitar, synthesizer and vocals; CE CE on bass, synthesizer and vocals; and Matt Chaikin on drums and percussion. Margaret Arana should not be carried over from the later lineup into this debut’s personnel list. The same LP record credits Steve Brenner as engineer, John Henning for mixing and John Golden for mastering. These are secondary pressing credits pending sleeve-level verification. [LP metadata](https://www.highfidelityla.com/release/9086272/kommunity-fk-the-vision-and-the-voice).
The useful distinction is live recording with few overdubs, not a claim that the debut has no synthesizers. Fasolino discusses synthesizer effects on the first album and a fuller keyboard role on Close One Sad Eye (1985). [Trouser Press](https://trouserpress.com/reviews/kommunity-fk/).
## Where to go next
Try Close One Sad Eye as the next chronological contrast; keep Something Inside Me Has Died attached to that 1985 record. The official discography confirms both original album years. It lists later debut editions separately, including Cleopatra 1994/1998 and Mobilization 2006. Other accounts use 1993 for the Cleopatra edition; that conflict remains visible. [Official discography](https://kommunityfk.wixsite.com/kommunityfk/discography).
