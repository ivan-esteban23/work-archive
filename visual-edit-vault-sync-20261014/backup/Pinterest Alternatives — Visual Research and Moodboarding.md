## A repeatable research workflow

1. **Define the visual question.** Choose one subject per board: a silhouette, fabric, styling detail, palette or page treatment.
2. **Collect with context.** Save the original page or creator post, image credit, publication date, country/city, and the styling detail you want to study.
3. **Compare references.** Use several independent examples. Keep runway, street photography, product listings, historical objects and AI interpretations visibly separate.
4. **Build the board.** Group by silhouette, material, palette and accessory. Add a sentence explaining each selection instead of collecting lookalikes without context.
5. **Keep the final record in Obsidian.** Store permitted local copies with the source record; revisit the original page before publication. A collection link alone is not a durable backup.

## Read the fashion landscape

- **Editorial direction.** Read i-D, The Gentlewoman or System for photography, styling and the editorial argument.
- **Market context.** Use Luxe.CO, Business of Fashion or FashionNetwork to investigate the business behind a visual signal.
- **Community signals.** Compare dated creator posts, outfit discussions and resale listings; avoid universal trend claims.
- **Historical grounding.** Use KCI, FIT and the Vintage Fashion Guild to distinguish a revival from a new silhouette.
- **Trace and monitor.** Trace image matches with TinEye, check historical captures and monitor available feeds with Inoreader.

Review sources weekly and synthesize monthly. Label each observation as editorial, market, community or historical evidence; record season and look identifiers where available.

Selected additions came from [[Attachments/Pinterest Alternatives/research_report_fashion_trends_media|the supplied research report]]. Prices, leadership claims, ownership changes, audience counts and blanket free-access claims were not imported.

## Research routes

### Prepare a photo shoot

Translate a mood into a usable brief.

1. **Define the story.** [The Gentlewoman](https://thegentlewoman.co.uk/) · [SHOWstudio](https://www.showstudio.com/). Choose a portrait mood, lighting direction and movement reference.
2. **Study the outfit.** [FASHIONSNAP](https://www.fashionsnap.com/streetstyle/) · [Vogue Runway](https://www.vogue.com/fashion-shows). Annotate silhouette, proportion and styling details from dated examples.
3. **Build the brief.** [PureRef](https://www.pureref.com/) · [Milanote](https://milanote.com/). Group references into light, wardrobe, location and poses; add credits and a shot list.

**Outcome:** A concise shoot brief with a credited board and a shot list.

### Research Asian fashion

Compare sources within each market.

1. **Choose the market.** [Vogue China](https://www.vogue.com.cn/) · [Vogue Korea](https://www.vogue.co.kr/) · [FASHIONSNAP](https://www.fashionsnap.com/streetstyle/). Start with one country, city or styling question.
2. **Compare the evidence.** [MUSINSA Snap](https://www.musinsa.com/snap/main/recommend) · [Tokyo Fashion](https://tokyofashion.com/photos/) · [Jing Daily](https://jingdaily.com/). Separate daily outfits, archived street photography and market reporting; check dates.
3. **Keep the source trail.** [Are.na](https://www.are.na/) · [Raindrop.io](https://raindrop.io/). Save original links and explain what each reference contributes.

**Outcome:** A dated comparison board with country, source type and styling notes.

### Design a magazine

Develop a consistent editorial system.

1. **Study the editorial rhythm.** [The Gentlewoman](https://thegentlewoman.co.uk/) · [Apartamento](https://www.apartamentomagazine.com/). Compare opening spreads, portrait pacing and relationships between text and images.
2. **Explore the design language.** [Magazine B](https://magazine-b.com/) · [Behance](https://www.behance.net/). Annotate grids, hierarchy, captions and image scale rather than copying a complete layout.
3. **Organize the direction.** [Are.na](https://www.are.na/) · [Milanote](https://milanote.com/). Create a type, palette and layout board, then sketch your own opening spread.

**Outcome:** A visual style sheet and an original spread concept.

### Identify vintage garments

Build an attribution from several clues.

1. **Record the object.** [Vintage Fashion Guild](https://vintagefashionguild.org/). Document labels, seams, fabric and construction before assigning a date.
2. **Compare historical context.** [FIT Fashion History Timeline](https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/) · [Kyoto Costume Institute](https://www.kci.or.jp/en/archives/). Check documented silhouettes and object dates; similarities are leads, not proof.
3. **Trace the image.** [TinEye](https://tineye.com/) · [Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org/). Investigate matching images and historical pages; keep the source record and uncertainty.

**Outcome:** An object record with evidence, a provisional date range and unresolved questions.
