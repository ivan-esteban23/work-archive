> [!danger] Korean second-hand is not thrift-bin pricing
> This is the thing to know before you shop. A **Barbour jacket at Garosu-gil runs ~₩100,000 (US$72)**, a **Burberry trench ~₩100,000–300,000 (US$72–220)**, and luxury pieces on the basement B1 floor go far higher. Hongdae's warehouse halls are the cheap tier at **₩15,000–60,000 (US$11–43)**. Gwangjang's second floor runs ₩20,000–300,000. None of it is bargain-hunting — it is a considered vintage market with real prices.
>
> **A ceiling decided on the street does not exist.** Set it, write it here, and hold it.

## 🧳 The real constraint is volume, not cost

> [!danger] Bring a foldable duffel, or plan to ship
> Six nights, three cities, two rail transfers. Vintage shopping fights the luggage you brought. Korea has **택배 courier from any convenience store** — buy a box, fill it, send it home, cheap and reliable next-day domestically.
>
> A soft bag you compress and repack handles a day's purchases. A hard case does not. If you buy more than fits, the courier is the answer, not the wardrobe.

> [!tip] Tax refund needs the passport you brought for the DMZ
> Immediate refund on purchases over **₩15,000** at Global Tax Free stores, with your physical passport. It reduces the cost of what you buy. It does not decide whether to buy it — the ceiling does.
