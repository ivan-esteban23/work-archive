**The phrases that actually get you through the week.** Not a phrasebook — a working set, ordered by when you will need it.

> [!tip] Print or screenshot the ten marked ⭐
> Everything below fits on one screen. The ⭐ marked ones cover the situations where being understood actually changes the outcome — the vegetarian requests, the emergency numbers, and the four you will use every single day.

## 📊 The ten that matter

| | Korean | Romanised | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ⭐ | **안녕하세요** | annyeonghaseyo | Hello — the one that opens anything |
| ⭐ | **감사합니다** | gamsahamnida | Thank you (formal) |
| ⭐ | **이거 뭐예요?** | igeo mwoyeyo? | What is this? — points at any menu or object |
| ⭐ | **간장 빼주세요** | ganjang ppajuseyo | **No soy sauce** — your most important sentence |
| ⭐ | **고기 육수 없이** | gogi yeoksu eopsi | No meat broth — for soups and stews |
| ⭐ | **얼마예요?** | eolmayeyo? | How much is this? |
| ⭐ | **계산할게요** | gyesanhalgeyo | I'll pay — when splitting |
| ⭐ | **화장실 어디예요?** | hwajangsil eodiyeyo? | Where is the toilet? |
| ⭐ | **112** | — | Emergency. **Police, fire and ambulance** |
| ⭐ | **1330** | — | 24-hour tourist helpline, multilingual |

> [!danger] Save both numbers before you land
> **112** and **1330**, in your phone, in Hangul as well as digits. **비상** means emergency. There is no reason to be looking these up when it matters.

## 🚶 Getting around

| Korean | Romanised | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 여기서요? | yeogiseoyo? | Where is it? (point at a map) |
| 화장실 | hwajangsil | Toilet |
| 지하철역 | jihacheollyeok | Subway station |
| 역 | yeok | Station |
| 몇 호선 | myeot hoseon | Which line? |
| **다음** | **daeum** | **Next** — for the train or bus |
| **정류장** | **jeongnyujang** | **Bus stop** |
| 여기서 세워주세요 | yeogiseyo sewojuseyo | Please stop here (in a taxi) |
| 좀 지나갈게요 | jom jinagalkeoyo | I'll be going past (declining a ride) |
| 멀어요 | meoreoyo | It's far |
| 가까워요 | kakkawoyo | It's near |
| 왼쪽 / 오른쪽 | oenjjok / oreunjjok | Left / right |
| 직진 | jikjin | Straight ahead |

> [!tip] Learn 다음 before anything else on the train
> **다음** (next) plus a pointed finger is how you get off at your stop. **내려요** (*naeryeoyo*, I'll get off) works too. Point at the door and say it — that is enough, every time.

### Dietary — the ones that matter

| Korean | Romanised | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ⭐ **간장 빼주세요** | **ganjang ppajuseyo** | **Leave out the soy sauce** |
| ⭐ **고기 육수 없이** | **gogi yeoksu eopsi** | **No meat broth** |
| 고기 안 돼요 | gogi an dwaeyo | No meat |
| 채식 가능해요? | chaesik ganeunghaeyo? | Is vegetarian possible? |
| 해산물 좋아해요 | haesaengmul joahaeyo | I like seafood |
| 물고기 | mulgogi | Fish — **not** octopus |
| 갑오징어 | gamojingeo | Octopus |
| 새우 | saewoo | Shrimp |
| 매운 것 빼주세요 | maeun geot ppajuseyo | Leave out the spicy things |
| 고수 빼주세요 | gosu ppajuseyo | No coriander |
| 양파 빼주세요 | yangpa ppajuseyo | No onion |

> [!danger] Why 간장 빼주세요 is the sentence that matters
> Korean soy sauce is very frequently made with **bonito or anchovy stock**. It is in nearly every stir-fry, stew and sauce in the country and it is **not always disclosed**. Saying **간장 빼주세요** is understood instantly, even with no Korean. Asking only for "no meat" is not enough — you will get a vegetable dish sitting in anchovy.
>
> **Watch the garnish too.** Dried anchovy flakes (*gaseo*) are placed on dishes as a garnish and routinely eaten. If there are small dried fish on the plate, leave them.

> [!tip] Show the words if you cannot say them
> Write on your phone: **간장 빼주세요** and **고기 육수 없이**. Hold the screen up. Every restaurant in Korea has dealt with this before and it works better than trying to pronounce it.

## 💰 Paying

| Korean | Romanised | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 얼마예요? | eolmayeyo? | How much? |
| 계산할게요 | gyesanhalgeyo | I'll pay |
| 같이 계산해 주세요 | gachi gyesanhae juseyo | Can we pay separately? |
| 카드로 할게요 | kadeuro halgeyo | By card |
| 현금으로 | hyeongeuro | In cash |
| 봉투 주세요 | bongtu juseyo | A bag, please |
| 이거 세탁해 주세요 | igeo setakhae juseyo | Dry-clean this, please |

> [!tip] Cash out at a 7-Eleven or GS25 ATM
> Card works widely in Seoul and Busan, but markets, temples and small shops are often cash-only. Carry **₩200,000–300,000**. Bank ATMs often need a Korean bank card; the convenience-store ones do not.

## 🛍 Shopping

| Korean | Romanised | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 얼마예요? | eolmayeyo? | How much? |
| 좀 깎아 주세요 | jom kkakka juseyo | Can you knock it down a bit? |
| 입어 봐도 돼요? | ibeo bwado dwaeyo? | Can I try this on? |
| 다른 색깔 있어요? | dareun saekkkal isseoyo? | Do you have another colour? |
| 영수증 주세요 | yeongsujeung juseyo | Receipt, please |
| 이거 보내줘요 | igeo bonaejuseyo | Send this (courier) |
| 사장님 | sajangnim | Owner — the standard address for anyone in a shop |

> [!tip] 사장님 works everywhere and means nothing
> It is how you call a shopkeeper — male, female, elderly, whoever. It is respectful and always welcome. **남자 사장님 / 여자 사장님** exists if you need to be specific, but you almost never do.

## 🆘 Need help

| Korean | Romanised | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ⭐ **112** | — | **Emergency — police, fire, ambulance** |
| ⭐ **1330** | — | **24-hour tourist helpline, English/Japanese/Chinese** |
| Help me | 도움 | dowum — help |
| 죄송합니다 | joesonghamnida | I'm sorry |
| 잠깐만요 | jamkkanmanyo | One moment, please |
| 괜찮아요? | gwaenchanayo? | Is it OK? / Are you all right? |
| 어디예요? | eodiyeyo? | Where is it? |
| Doctor | 병원 | byeongwon — hospital |
| Pharmacy | 약국 | yakguk |

> [!danger] If you need a hospital, show the words
> **병원** (byeongwon) is a hospital; **약국** (yakguk) is a pharmacy. If you are unwell enough to need one, showing these two words on your phone does more than any attempt to explain symptoms in English.

## 🙏 Small courtesies

| Korean | Romanised | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 안녕히 계세요 | annyeonghi gyeseyo | Goodbye (to someone staying) |
| 안녕히 가세요 | annyeonghi gaseyo | Goodbye (to someone leaving) |
| 감사합니다 | gamsahamnida | Thank you (formal) |
| 고마워요 | gomawoyo | Thanks (casual) |
| 죄송합니다 | joesonghamnida | I'm sorry |
| 괜찮습니다 | gwaenchansseumnida | It's fine |
| 잠시만요 | jamsiman-yo | Just a moment |
| 네 / 아니요 | ne / aniyo | Yes / No |

> [!tip] The two different goodbyes will confuse you for a day
> **안녕히 가세요** goes to the person *leaving*; **안녕히 계세요** goes to the person *staying*. Get them backwards and nobody minds at all. Use whichever you remember.

## 🔢 Numbers

| | | | | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 il | 2 i | 3 sam | 4 sa | 5 o |
| 6 yuk | 7 chil | 8 pal | 9 gu | 10 sip |

Prices are spoken more often than written, and **many Koreans use sino-Korean numerals in shops**: 1,500 is **천오백** (*cheon-o-baek*), not *il-oh-baek*. Either works, but the sino form is what you will hear.

> [!tip] You will not need most of this
> Everyone in tourist areas — and honestly most people in Korea in general — handles English, and the metro and shops are used to foreigners. **The exception that matters is dietary**, because it needs precision rather than charm. Learn the ten ⭐ phrases, carry them on your phone, and you are better equipped than most visitors.
