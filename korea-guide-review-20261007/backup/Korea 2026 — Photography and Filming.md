# 📷 Korea 2026 — Photography and filming

[[Korea 2026 — Start Here|← Dashboard]] · [[Korea 2026 — Autumn Foliage|Autumn foliage]] · [[Korea 2026 — Itinerary|Day by day]] · [[Korea 2026 — My Five Things|Five things]] · [[Korea 2026 — Daily Pocket Cards|Pocket cards]]

**Creative plan · added 7 October 2026.** Built on the 27 October – 2 November itinerary. Light suggestions are preferences, not promises — autumn light in Seoul changes hour to hour. **One strong photo and one short sequence a day, not a hundred frames.**

> [!danger] If you are bringing a drone, read this first
> Flying unapproved in a restricted zone in Korea now risks **one year in prison or a ₩10,000,000 fine.** Roughly **99% of Seoul's airspace is restricted**, the DMZ is absolutely prohibited, and airport control zones extend **9.3 km** — which covers Incheon, Gimpo and Gimhae. Registration for anything over 250 g needs a Korean mobile number, which visitors do not have. **The realistic options are a designated drone park or hiring a licensed Korean operator.** Full detail in [[Korea 2026 — Practical Info#🚁 Drones — read this before flying anything|the drones section]]. If you are not bringing one, skip all of this.

## 🎯 My three priorities

- [ ] **One autumn photo I love** — a wide scene, a reflection, or a close detail. Not four mediocre ones.
- [ ] **One listening-room hour** — a record playing in a room, in a city that does this after dark. [[Korea 2026 — Cafés and Shops|Wanita]], ₩16,500 including the drink.
- [ ] **One 30–90 second film** — autumn colour, a city or temple detail, food or tea, and one human moment.

These feed [[Korea 2026 — My Five Things]]. A finished personal memory counts even if nothing is posted anywhere.

---

## 🎒 What to carry

| Item | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Fast prime, 35 or 50 mm equivalent** | Gyeongbokgung's gates, Bulguksa's terraces and Wolji Pond's reflections all reward a normal-to-short focal length. This is your main lens |
| **Wide, 16–24 mm equivalent** | Only for Gamcheon's 3.5 m alleys and Ikseon-dong's hanok lanes. Bring it, leave it in the bag |
| **Spare batteries — more than you think** | Cold drains them. October mornings at Bulguksa and Haedong Yonggungsa sit near 10°C, and you will be shooting before sunrise and after dark |
| **A small cleaning cloth** | Ginkgo dust and street haze; Seoul air carries fine particulate |
| **Rain cover** | Brief October showers arrive without warning and mist your front element |

> [!tip] One lens is enough if you must choose
> The 35 mm equivalent. Seoul's best autumn frames — palace gates, stone walls, narrow alleys, a lit bridge — are all read at that focal length. The wide is a convenience, not a necessity.

## Day 1 · Tue 27 Oct — Ikseon-dong

**Slot:** [[Korea 2026 — Pocket 2026-10-27|27 October]], mid-afternoon.

- [ ] **Photo:** Ikseon-dong's 1930s hanok blocks — rooflines against sky, air-conditioning units and wires on old timber. Repeat the same corner in a tighter frame to show the contrast.
- [ ] **Film:** one slow lateral move along the Gyeongui Line Forest Park walkway looking down at the rail lines. 8–12 seconds, keep it steady.
- [ ] **Detail:** *Dalkom*'s walnut cake, or the walnut pieces themselves — a texture frame to break up the architecture.

**Light:** flat and diffuse mid-afternoon, which is genuinely good here — soft light suits weathered timber. No harsh shadows to fight. Avoid shooting straight into the low sun if it is west of the alley.

**Missed the light?** The evening at Gwangjang Market gives you warm interior glow, steam off the griddles, and backlit food — the best available substitute.

---

## Day 2 · Wed 28 Oct — Palaces and the ginkgo wall

**Slot:** [[Korea 2026 — Pocket 2026-10-28|28 October]], 09:30–13:00.

- [ ] **Gyeongbokgung at 09:30:** the gate and courtyard light. Compose the gate from the axis for scale, then step inside for a compressed frame through the doorway onto the throne hall.
- [ ] **Deoksugung stone wall path:** ginkgo and zelkova along the palace wall, running into Jeongdong-gil. This is the most photographed hundred metres of autumn in Korea — and it is at its best in **late afternoon warm light**, which you have at roughly 17:00 if you skip the Hongdae loop.
- [ ] **Film:** a stationary wide of the wall path with leaves moving, then one close-up of a branch. 5–10 seconds per clip.
- [ ] **Optional, if you have the light:** Deoksugung itself, open **until 21:00** (₩1,000, last entry 20:00). The only palace you can photograph lit this trip.

> [!tip] The guard-change ceremony — go at 11:00 or 13:00, not 10:00 or 14:00
> The Royal Guard Changing Ceremony at Gwanghwamun runs at 10:00 and 14:00, and both slots are shoulder to shoulder with tour groups. **Arrive at 11:00 or 13:00** — same ceremony, a fraction of the people, and the architectural scale of Gwanghwamun is still there. This is the single best crowd-avoidance tip in Seoul.

**Light:** morning within the courtyards for long shadows; the wall path late afternoon. Full seasonal timing in [[Korea 2026 — Autumn Foliage|the foliage note]].

> ⚠️ **Deoksugung and Changdeokgung close on Mondays.** Gyeongbokgung closes Tuesdays. You are here on a **Wednesday** — all open. See [[Korea 2026 — Practical Info#🚪 Palace and site closure days|the closure table]].

## Day 3 · Thu 29 Oct — The DMZ

**Slot:** [[Korea 2026 — Pocket 2026-10-29|29 October]], 08:30–12:30.

- [ ] **Imjingak:** the reunification monuments, especially Mangbaedan altar where families separated in 1950 still pray north. Shoot low and respectful — this is not a photo opportunity in the usual sense.
- [ ] **Third Infiltration Tunnel:** the descent and the barricaded end. **Photography is prohibited inside the tunnel** — the walk is 265 m, 1.95 m high, 2 m wide, stepped and steep, with mandatory helmets. Shoot the exhibit hall and the approach instead, and let the tunnel itself be remembered rather than photographed.
- [ ] **Dora Observatory:** binocular view toward Kijŏng-dong. A frame through the binocular housing, or the telescope itself with the view beyond. Clear days only — this is the one shot that depends on weather you cannot schedule.
- [ ] **Jeongdong Night Walk, 19:00:** the ginkgo wall lit. Best frame is at **blue hour**, roughly 18:00–18:30, before the lighting takes over. Come from the Deoksugung side and walk toward City Hall.

**Light:** hard cold sun with low contrast on the DMZ — the clearest air of the year, and everything is sharp. Bring gloves that work on a tripod or monopod; your hands will not want to.

> [!tip] Shoot the wall before the lights, not after
> The ginkgo along the Deoksugung wall is far more photogenic at 17:00 than under the night programme's lighting. You walk this same wall on Day 2 — if the light is good on Wednesday, take the daylight frame then and treat Thursday evening as atmosphere only.

## Day 4 · Fri 30 Oct — Gyeongju, and the anchor image

**Slot:** [[Korea 2026 — Pocket 2026-10-30|30 October]], 20:30–22:00.

> [!tip] This is the trip's defining image — protect the time for it
> **Wolji Pond at dusk.** The pavilions reflected in still water, Silla stonework lit from below. **Bring the fast prime, not the wide.** A normal focal length compresses the pavilions across the water; a wide makes the pond look empty. This needs a tripod or a stable surface, a long-ish exposure, and patience for the lamps to come up. ₩3,000, last entry 21:30.

- [ ] **Photo:** the Wolji reflection first. Then Daereungwon's tomb mounds in late afternoon — long shadows across Silla grass, which reads better than the flat midday version.
- [ ] **Woljeongyo Bridge:** the restored wooden bridge lit against the sky. Shoot from the bank, low, with the reflection.
- [ ] **Film:** one slow static frame of the pond as the lamps come on, then the bridge. 10–15 seconds. Do not pan fast — still water and slow moves suit a tripod.
- [ ] **Detail:** Eoseoseo's hand-stamped bookmark, or Bitgguri's grain syrup and rice cake. Food as texture.

**Light:** dusk, so expose for the lamps and let the sky go slightly blue. Arrive by 20:15 to be settled before full dark.

> [!warning] Dress for it
> Gyeongju nights are **5–9°C** in late October — the coldest of the three cities. Gloves, and a tripod if you have one. This is the one block where you will want to stand still in the dark for ten minutes.

## Day 5 · Sat 31 Oct — Bulguksa

**Slot:** [[Korea 2026 — Pocket 2026-10-31|31 October]], 08:30–10:15.

- [ ] **Photo:** Bulguksa's stone terraces with the pagodas — Dabotap and Seokgatap, thirteen centuries of the same subject. Shoot the terraces wide with the mountain behind, then compress to the Dabotap tiers.
- [ ] **Seokguram (optional):** the stone Buddha in its grotto. A tight frame on the face, then wide for the chamber. Skip it if you are tired — it is a 20-minute uphill bus on top of a full morning.
- [ ] **Film:** one slow approach along the stone terrace path, 8–10 seconds, with the pagoda resolving as you move.
- [ ] **Busan arrival:** Yongdusan cable car over the harbour at golden hour. Wide, because this is genuinely a wide scene — container port, Haeundae Bridge, city.

**Light:** 08:30 is the point. Low sun raking across the terraces, long shadows, and an empty temple. By 10:00 the tour buses have arrived and the light has flattened.

---

## Day 6 · Sun 1 Nov — Gamcheon and Millak-dong

**Slot:** [[Korea 2026 — Pocket 2026-11-01|1 November]], 09:45–11:15.

> [!tip] Wide lens, and the 09:45 start, both matter
> Gamcheon's lanes are **3.5 metres wide with 300+ steps**. This is the one place on the trip that genuinely needs the wide lens. **At 09:45 you get the light and the quiet; after lunch it is shoulder to shoulder.**

- [ ] **Photo:** look up, not down. The best frames in Gamcheon are vertical — stacked houses, laundry lines, sky between roofs. Shoot the same stairway wide and then tight to show the compression.
- [ ] **Millak-dong:** the painted alley walls — yellow, mint, green, blue — are a colour palette unique to this city. Flat, even, no-direction light. Easy frames that look better than they should.
- [ ] **Haedong Yonggungsa:** the sea-cliff temple with waves through the courtyard. Frame the dragon windows against white water; a slow shutter turns the surf into mist.
- [ ] **Gwangalli at sunset:** the bridge and the bay. Golden hour, then blue hour — hold for both.
- [ ] **Film:** one continuous walk down a Gamcheon stairway, 10–15 seconds, no cuts. The handheld descent sells the terrain.

**Light:** mid-morning in Gamcheon is even and forgiving. Sunset over Gwangalli is the day's second peak — do not miss it if you cut Haedong.

- [ ] **Before boarding:** back up everything to a second location. Note what you would shoot differently.

## 🚫 What you cannot photograph

| Place | Restriction |
| --- | --- |
| **Third Infiltration Tunnel** | **No photography inside.** Helmets mandatory; stepped, narrow, steep |
| **The DMZ generally** | Photography is restricted around military installations and the Military Demarcation Line. Follow your guide's instructions every time, without exception |
| Imjingak | Not prohibited, but be restrained. Mangbaedan is an altar, not an attraction |
| Museums | Some prohibit flash; several prohibit tripods |
| Temples | Check signage. Bow and step aside rather than pushing for a frame |
| Changdeokgung Secret Garden | **Not on this itinerary** — timed guided entry, and hanbok entry does not cover the garden |
| Foreigners photographing foreigners' faces | Ask first. One second of politeness removes the whole issue |

> [!warning] Bongeunsa Temple, Gangnam — a specific close-down case
> Foreign visitors photographing **nuns** at Bongeunsa have been targeted for ending the temple's foreign-friendly reputation. It has closed to groups. **Do not photograph nuns or monks anywhere.** This is a live risk to the site itself, not just an etiquette point.

## 🎬 A simple film sequence

Six clips, about 70 seconds cut together. One per day.

| Day | Clip | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Ikseon-dong rooflines, then Gwangjang steam | 10s |
| 2 | Gyeongbokgung gate, morning light · wall path leaves | 12s |
| 3 | Tunnel entrance steps · Dora's view · the lit wall at dusk | 12s |
| 4 | **Wolji Pond, lamps coming on** · Woljeongyo reflected | 15s |
| 5 | Bulguksa terraces at 08:30 · Busan harbour golden hour | 12s |
| 6 | One continuous walk down a Gamcheon stairway | 15s |

**Rules that keep it watchable:** one subject per clip, never pan faster than you can hold steady, no music-video edits, and let silence sit. A travel film with two seconds of a real pause is worth more than one cut every second.

> [!quote] On collecting versus finishing
> Choose **one photo and one sequence a day**. You will come home with seven that you love rather than four hundred that are technically fine. The seventh night is the one to stop shooting and write down what the trip was — [[Korea 2026 — My Five Things|what it was actually for]].
