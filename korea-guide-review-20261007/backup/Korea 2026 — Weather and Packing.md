## 🌡️ What to expect

| City | Day | Night | Character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Seoul** | 12–16°C | **6–10°C** | Inland. Coldest nights, strongest wind on the DMZ coach and Inwangsan |
| **Gyeongju** | 14–18°C | **5–9°C** | Inland basin. The coldest of the three — open dawn at Bulguksa |
| **Busan** | 18–20°C | 11–13°C | Coast. Mildest; the ocean is still holding warmth |

**Base data:** Seoul's October averages are 19°C high / 9°C low across 11 years of records, with roughly 8.3 hours of daily sunshine. Late-October sits below that. The Korea Meteorological Administration's outlook, published 31 July 2026, put a **50% chance that October runs warmer than the 13.9–14.7°C normal**, with precipitation near or below normal.

**Rain:** October is Seoul's **driest month** — three to seven days, mostly brief showers. Typhoon risk by 27 October is effectively zero.

> [!warning] The wettest-looking window is 20–22 October, just before you land
> Current model output suggests overcast and then rain across 20–22 October. That is before departure, but a wet spell often carries a few days. **Check the ten-day forecast on 22 October** and lock the rain fallback from [[Korea 2026 — Itinerary|the itinerary]] then.

## 🍂 Foliage — what you will actually see

| Peak date, 2026 forecast | Where | Your dates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First colour ~23 Oct | Bukhansan | Just starting on arrival |
| Ginkgo **~30 Oct** | Nationwide, incl. Seoul streets | Turning on 28–29 Oct — **not full gold** |
| Maple **~31 Oct** | Nationwide | Turning, not peaked |
| Bukhansan peak ~4 Nov | Seoul's ridge | Missed by a week |
| Downtown parks | Enter peak from ~24 Oct | **This is your window** |
| Seoraksan peak ~25 Oct | Gangwon | Not visiting |

**Honest framing:** you will catch the trees **turning**, not at peak. Ginkgo is Seoul's street tree in numbers — 34.7% of the city's stock — so the gold arrives all at once on the boulevards during your stay. Downtown parks are entering peak. Bukhansan's ridges have started. If a warm autumn pulls everything forward two weeks, this window looks mid-peak instead, which is still a large improvement on the original dates.

> [!tip] Seoul's best-value foliage, for these dates
> **Seoul Forest** (Seongsu) — metasequoia and a walkable park next to the best coffee district · **Inwangsan** — quiet, short, old fortress walls, gorgeous at dusk, a tenth of Bukhansan's people · **Olympic Park** — sprawling enough that crowding stays manageable. Full detail in [[Korea 2026 — Itinerary|Day 2]].

## ✅ Packing checklist

**Clothing**
- [ ] **Warm jacket** — genuinely warm, not a midweight. This is the single most important item
- [ ] Thermal base layer (one is enough; you will wear it under the jacket on the DMZ morning and at Bulguksa)
- [ ] **Gloves** — you will be outside at Wolji Pond after 20:30 and again on the Jeongdong walk
- [ ] Warm socks, two pairs you can change
- [ ] Light scarf — for the Inwangsan evening and the Bulguksa approach
- [ ] Comfortable walking shoes, **already broken in**. Gamcheon's alleys are 3.5 m wide with 300+ steps
- [ ] **Second pair of shoes** if you own them. Day 6 is five blocks on foot

**Camera and electronics**
- [ ] **Spare camera batteries** — cold drains them far faster than heat, and mornings at Bulguksa and Haedong Yonggungsa are cold
- [ ] Fast prime (35 or 50 mm equivalent) for Ikseon-dong's alleys and Wolji's reflections
- [ ] Wide lens if you have one, for Gamcheon's corridors
- [ ] **Type C/F adapter** — Korea is 220V, 60Hz
- [ ] Power bank; a full day of photos is a heavy draw

**Health and weather**
- [ ] Compact umbrella — October showers arrive without warning and are mostly brief
- [ ] Light rain shell, if you own one and dislike umbrella weather
- [ ] Sunglasses — October light is low and clean; it glares off stone and water
- [ ] Anything you normally need, in a day bag rather than in checked luggage

**Documents**
- [ ] **Passport** — needed physically for the DMZ on 29 Oct, and again for tax refund. Six months' validity
- [ ] Travel insurance details offline on your phone
- [ ] Accommodation addresses saved in Korean as well as English

> [!tip] Photograph the Korean name of your hotel and save it offline
> A taxi driver reads Korean, not English. Save the address as an image on your phone and show it. This saves a confusing ten minutes at the first arrival.

## 🎒 What you are deliberately not bringing

| Left behind | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Umbrella stand, heavy books | Buy locally or ship. Second-hand Korea is cheaper than bringing it |
| The whole wardrobe | See the shopping note — vintage here is not cheap, but Korea has courier shipping. Buy and send |
| Heavy camera bag | Six nights, three cities, two rail transfers. A small bag you actually carry beats a large one you resent |

> [!warning] Shopping creates a luggage problem, not a budget one
> The risk on this trip is volume, not cost. Bring a **foldable duffel** for purchases, or plan to buy a box and use Korean courier (택배) from any convenience store to send it home. See [[Korea 2026 — Cafés and Shops|practical notes]].
