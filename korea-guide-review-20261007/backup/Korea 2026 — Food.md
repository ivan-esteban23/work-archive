# 🍽 Korea 2026 — Food

[[Korea 2026 — Start Here|← Dashboard]] · [[Korea 2026 — Itinerary|Where each meal falls]] · [[Korea 2026 — Cafés and Shops|Cafés]] · [[Korea 2026 — Budget|Budget]]

**One traveler avoids meat. Fish is fine.** That is a much easier brief than vegetarian proper, and it opens up most of coastal Busan. Every recommendation below carries a flag.

| Flag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ✔ | **Veg-friendly as ordered.** Nothing to ask, nothing to check |
| ◐ | **Veg possible, but you must say so.** Plain-language request works; check the sauce |
| ◑ | **Fish or seafood dish.** Fine for this trip |
| ✗ | **Meat or fish stock. Skip** |

> [!danger] One rule that solves most of Korea without meat
> **간장 (ganjang), the brown soy sauce, is frequently made with bonito or anchovy stock.** It is in almost every stir-fry, stew and sauce in the country, and it is not always disclosed. Say **"간장 빼주세요"** — *please leave out the soy sauce* — and you will be understood instantly, even with no Korean. Asking for "no meat" alone is not enough.

## Day 1 · Tue 27 Oct — Arrival and Ikseon-dong

| Meal | Place | What | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brunch | *Makdong Toseong*, Junghwang-dong | **Gukbap** — rice soup. Ask for the **vegetable pot**; it is a genuine dish, not an afterthought. Halal kitchen | ✔ |
| Café | *Dalkom*, Ikseon-dong | Walnut cake. Go early or it sells out | ✔ |
| Dinner | **Gwangjang Market** | **Bindaetteok** — mung-bean pancake, fried on the spot · **Tteokbokki** — rice cakes in sweet chilli. At the older stalls, ask plainly for no meat and no saké in the sauce | ✔ ◐ |

**Backup** — *Eonnae Handaxe* (은애한식), Euljiro. Banchan-forward — a dozen small vegetable dishes before anything else arrives. The single easiest vegetarian meal in Seoul.

**The market is also a shop.** Go up two floors after eating; see [[Korea 2026 — Cafés and Shops|Gwangjang 2F vintage]].

## Day 2 · Wed 28 Oct — Palaces, books, Hongdae

| Meal | Place | What | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lunch | *Sajae* (사재), Samcheong-dong | Traditional **jeongshik** set menu — rice, soup, several banchan. Trivially made vegetable-forward | ✔ |
| Dinner | Yeonnam-dong | **Jejuga** (제주고) if you want a Jeju-style rice bowl, or any *jeongshik* room on the street | ✔ |
| Late | **책익다**, Seochon | Bookshop-bar. Whisky or a Korean soju with a book. Not a meal — an ending | ✔ |

**Alternative** — **Jejugo** (제주고), Insadong: Jeju rice bowl, very good without meat.

> [!tip] Buy one banchan-heavy lunch and copy it
> Korean set menus are assembled, not fixed. Order the rice and soup, then ask for the vegetable sides. What comes is whatever the kitchen made that morning — usually four or five plates of genuinely good banchan, for ₩12,000–18,000.

| Dinner | *Eonnae Handaxe* (은애한식), Euljiro | As Day 1's backup. Banchan-led | ✔ |

**DMZ vegetarian swap, if you use it** — **Baegyangsa Temple**, Imjingang. **Sot bap**, temple rice cooked in oil reserved for monastic use, ₩12,000 by donation. It is the most interesting vegetarian meal in Korea and it is a temple, not a restaurant. **Reserve with the temple a day ahead.**

## Day 4 · Fri 30 Oct — Gyeongju

| Meal | Place | What | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lunch | *Hwangnam Gukbap* (황남국밥) | **Sarangchae** — the defining Gyeongju meal: nine to twelve small vegetable dishes around rice | ✔ |
| Snack | Hwangnidan-gil | **Hwangnam-pp** (황남빵) — butter-crust bread. The street exists because of this | ✔ |
| Dinner | *Ssanggye Sikdang* (쌍계식당) | The legendary local room. **Hobachui** — abalone braised in soy and sake | ◑ |
| Dinner, veg | **Omma Temple** restaurant | Buddhist temple cooking, ₩12,000. **Reserve the day before** | ✔ |

> [!tip] Ssanggye, if you want it without meat
> Order *maeuntak* for the table and build your own plate from the banchan. The room is generous about it and nobody minds — but the reason most people come is the chicken, so say what you need up front rather than picking around it.

**Also worth it** — **Gyeongju Wonjo Kongguk** (경주원조공국): soybean soup in a family line since 1956, and it keeps **vegan options**. Good breakfast before the tombs.

## Day 5 · Sat 31 Oct — Bulguksa to Busan

| Meal | Place | What | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lunch | Hwangnam-dong | Back to **Hwangnam Gukbap**, or *Ssanggye* if you held out | ✔ |
| Dinner | *Jejuga*, Gwangbok-dong | Traditional Busan set. Very vegetable-forward — Busan eats a lot of *namul* | ✔ |
| Alternative | *Daeun Haejangguk*, Seomyeon | **Milmyu** — milky radish rice-cake soup, the dish Busan is known for | ◑ **fish broth** |

> [!tip] Milmyu is the one place to make a judgement call
> It is arguably the defining Busan dish and it is built on **fish broth**. You said fish is fine, so this is a recommendation rather than a question — but it is the single meal in the trip where your preference actually changes the dish rather than the garnish.

## 🥡 Drinks and snacks worth knowing

| What | Where | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Makgeolli** (막걸리) | Markets, pojangmacha | Milky, sour, cheap, often sold in a bowl. The default Korean drink and nobody thinks twice about it |
| **Soju** (소주) | Anywhere | Pineapple and green grape are the easy ones |
| **Citrus tea** (유자차) | Cafés, temple towns | Warm, honeyed, and the standard thing to order when you cannot face more coffee |
| **Milleffeuille, bungeoppang** | Cafés, bakeries | **Bungeoppang** is the fish-shaped bun stuffed with custard — a Busan street signature |
| **Hotteok** | Night markets | Cinnamon sugar, best in cold weather. Yours is cold enough |

## 🔑 Phrases that solve the problem

| Korean | Meaning | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 간장 빼주세요 | Please leave out the soy sauce | **The one that matters** |
| 고기 안 돼요 | No meat | Understandable everywhere |
| 채식 가능해요? | Is vegetarian possible? | At a counter with no menu |
| 이거 안 맵게 해주세요 | Not spicy, please | Order spicy food without it |
| 물 좀 주세요 | Water, please | — |
| 계산할게요 | I'll pay | When splitting |
| 사진 찍어도 돼요? | May I take a photo? | Worth asking in temples and shops |

> [!info] Ganseul (간선) vs ganjang (간장) — do not confuse them
> **간선** is dried salted pollock and looks like a fish. **간장** is soy sauce. You are refusing the second, not the first. Saying "간장 빼주세요" is the single most useful sentence in this note.

---

> [!warning] Ask about stock in soups and stews
> Meat stock in a "vegetable" soup is common and rarely volunteered. The requests above handle fried and sauced dishes. For **jjigae, guk and soup**, add **고기 육수 없이** — *no meat broth*.
