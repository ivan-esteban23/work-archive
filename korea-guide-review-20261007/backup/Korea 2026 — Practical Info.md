## 🛂 Entry and the DMZ

| Requirement | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Passport** | Physical document, six months' validity. Copies are not accepted at DMZ checkpoints |
| **Visa** | Chilean citizens currently enter visa-free for short stays. **Verify before departure** — this is the one thing that must be checked against the Korean immigration site, not a travel blog |
| **K-ETA** | Not required for visa-exempt short-term tourism. Confirm current status before you fly |
| **Arrival card** | Fill the online arrival card before landing; it saves 20 minutes at immigration |
| **DMZ access** | Passport **in hand**, plus the tour confirmation. Checked at a military checkpoint before entering the civilian control line |

> [!danger] Verify the visa position yourself
> Everything else in this note is a planning convenience. **Entry requirements are the one item where an outdated blog can cost you a flight.** Check the Korean Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the embassy for Chile in the fortnight before you travel, and screenshot anything you might need offline.

## 🚪 Palace and site closure days

The palaces do **not** share a closing day, and this is the most common avoidable mistake in Seoul.

| Site | Closed | Cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gyeongbokgung** | **Tuesdays** | ₩3,000, or free in hanbok | Largest. Also closed the third Tuesday monthly for a guard ceremony |
| **Changdeokgung** | **Mondays** | ₩3,000 + ₩5,000 Secret Garden | UNESCO. Secret Garden is timed guided entry only |
| **Deoksugung** | **Mondays** | ₩1,000 | **Open until 21:00**, last entry 20:00 — your only after-dark palace |
| **Changgyeonggung** | **Mondays** | ₩1,000 | Pairs with the Secret Garden |
| Bulguksa | — | Free per VisitKorea (older tables ₩6,000) | Carries ₩10,000 |
| Haedong Yonggungsa | — | Free | No fixed weekly closure |

**You land Tuesday 27 October — the one day Gyeongbokgung is shut.** The itinerary visits on Wednesday the 28th, so it works as written, but anything that slides earlier hits a locked gate. Confirm in the week before you fly.

> [!warning] Gyeongbokgung's autumn night viewing ended 2 October 2026
> The September lantern event finished before the colour arrived, so it is gone. For a palace after dark in your window, **Deoksugung is the answer** — open to 21:00, and the ginkgo wall outside it is the thing being lit anyway. See [[Korea 2026 — Photography and Filming|photography notes]].

> [!warning] Two passport checks, same trip
> The DMZ on 29 October and tax-refund purchases both need the physical document. Keep it with you rather than in checked luggage — which it will be, since you carry it.

## 🎃 Halloween weekend, 27 Oct – 1 Nov

> [!info] You are not in Seoul when it matters — but know the context
> **Halloween 2026 falls on Saturday 31 October**, the first time since 2022 that the date itself lands on a weekend night. Expect the largest crowds and the tightest controls of the year.
>
> **Your route already handles it.** Your Seoul nights are **27, 28 and 29 October** — before the busy stretch. The 30th and 31st are **Gyeongju and Busan**. **There is no collision.** You are in the quietest place in the country when Seoul is at its most managed.

**Why this note exists anyway:** if you ever re-plan, move a day, or are simply asked about it, the context matters.

### The history you should know before going anywhere near Itaewon

On **29 October 2022, 159 people died** in a crowd crush in a narrow sloping alley beside the Hamilton Hotel in Itaewon. **26 of the victims were foreign nationals.** Since then, Seoul runs Halloween as a public-safety operation: police and district staff on main streets, **one-way pedestrian flows** in the busiest lanes, **subway exits closed** when platforms fill, and real-time crowd-density monitoring.

> [!danger] If you are ever in Seoul during this window
> **If you are told to move or a street is too full to move freely in, leave.** Do not stop in a packed alley for a photograph. In 2025, police closed the memorial alley itself from 22:20 and **trains skipped Itaewon Station from 23:00**, with passengers directed to Noksapyeong or Hangangjin — each about ten minutes' walk on Line 6. Know those two backup stations before you go.

### Where it happens, and where it doesn't

| Place | 2026 status |
| --- | --- |
| **Hongdae** | **Where the crowd actually goes.** ~110,000 people in the area by 22:00 last year. Cosplayers, street performers, queues outside every bar. Police-managed with barriers and one-way lanes |
| **Itaewon** | **No organised celebration since 2022.** Most businesses keep decorations down. The crush alley is now the **10·29 Path of Memory and Safety** — "we still have names to remember" set into the ground, with information panels. People leave white chrysanthemums |
| **Theme parks** | The organised, ticketed version. **Lotte World** (to 15 Nov) — Junji Ito collaboration, night parade 20:20, mostly indoors, on the subway from central Seoul. **Everland** (to 22 Nov) — *Blood City Zero*, a tenth-year immersive zombie zone, 16:00–21:00, included with entry |

> [!warning] Visit the Itaewon memorial quietly, and not in costume
> Families have asked visitors to come quietly and **not in costume**. If you go, walk past it and keep moving. No photographs of the memorial panels.

> [!danger] Wearing a police costume is a criminal offence
> Dressing as a police officer — uniform or otherwise recognisable — is **illegal in Korea for anyone who is not an officer**: up to **six months in prison or a ₩3,000,000 fine**. Police issue warnings about it every year. This applies in Seoul and everywhere else in the country.

### If you wanted the theme parks

Both would cost you a Seoul day. **Lotte World** is the practical one — on Lines 2 and 8 at Jamsil, mostly indoors so weather-proof, and the 20:20 parade is the payoff. ₩55,000–67,000 adult. **Everland** is an hour south by bus, fuller-day, ₩46,000–68,000.

> [!tip] Honestly — skip it on this trip
> This itinerary is built around Wolji Pond after dark, an empty Bulguksa at 08:30, and a hill village before the crowds. A costume street party is a different holiday, the 31st is already a **transfer day into Busan**, and you will be tired from two nights of rail. **If you want a Halloween, the honest answer is to plan one deliberately in a different month** — not to graft it onto this one.

## 💳 Money

| | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Cards** | Visa and Mastercard work widely. Smaller stalls and temples are cash-only |
| **Cash** | Carry ₩200,000–300,000 for markets, temples, small shops and taxis. See [[Korea 2026 — Food|markets and temples]] |
| **ATM** | 7-Eleven and GS25 convenience stores have working ATMs. Bank ATMs often need a Korean bank card |
| **Exchange** | Better to withdraw from an ATM on arrival than to exchange before you fly |
| **Conversion** | Note your CLP rate and date when you set the ceiling in [[Korea 2026 — Budget|Budget]] |

**Planning rate used here: ₩1,380 = US$1.** Real rates move — convert once, on a recorded date, and stop recalculating.

> [!tip] Tipping does not exist and is not expected
> Nobody tips in Korea. Staff will be genuinely confused by it, and it can create an awkward transaction. Just pay and go.

### Tax refund

Purchases over **₩15,000** at Global Tax Free participating shops qualify for immediate refund at the airport, **with your passport**. It applies to the Korea portion only. It reduces the cost of what you buy — it does not decide whether to buy it.

## 📱 Phone and connectivity

| Option | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **eSIM before departure** | Cheapest and simplest. Buy on the plane Wi-Fi if your provider sells one there |
| Roaming SIM at ICN | More expensive but works; buy at the airport, not the city |
| Pocket Wi-Fi rental | Usually overkill for two phones |
| Free Wi-Fi | Common in airports, large malls and many cafés. Ask for the password, don't assume it is open |

**Useful apps:** **Naver Map** and **KakaoMap** for Korean-language navigation (both far better than Google Maps inside Korea — Google routing is unreliable on local roads). **Papago** for translation. **Subway Korea** or **Navitime** for metro routing.

> [!tip] Google Maps works, but it is not the local option
> Google Maps struggles with Korean addresses and transit directions inside the country. Search links in this guide still use Google Maps because they resolve reliably from abroad — but once you are navigating day to day, **Naver Map** is what locals use and it handles Korean address systems properly. Install both before landing.

## 🛍 Shopping and shipping

| | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Immediate tax refund** | Over ₩15,000, Global Tax Free shops, passport required |
| **Duty free** | Only on departure; not useful for a six-night trip |
| **Best vintage tier** | Hongdae / Yeonnam, ₩15,000–80,000 |
| **Curated luxury tier** | Garosu-gil, ₩30,000–300,000+ |
| **Shipping home** | **택배 courier from any convenience store.** Buy a box, fill it, send it. Next-day domestic, cheap |
| **Duty on a parcel** | Small personal parcels to Chile may attract duty; check your postal terms before shipping a lot |

> [!tip] Bring a foldable duffel, not an empty suitcase
> The constraint on this trip is volume, not weight. A soft bag you can compress and repack handles a day of vintage purchases; a hard case does not. If you buy more than fits, the courier is genuinely cheap and reliable.

## 🚁 Drones — read this before flying anything

> [!danger] If you own a drone: assume you cannot legally fly it here
> Korea tightened penalties for unauthorised no-fly-zone flights in **September 2026**. Flying unapproved in a restricted zone now carries **up to one year in prison or a ₩10,000,000 fine** (~$7,200) — raised from an administrative fine of ₩3,000,000. Fines of up to ₩5,000,000 are also in use near airports. **This is not a theoretical risk.**

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Seoul airspace** | Roughly **99% is restricted**, under Capital Defense Command. Includes tourist landmarks. Special military authorisation required regardless of drone weight |
| **DMZ** | Absolutely prohibited |
| **Airport control zones** | Extend **9.3 km** from any airport — which covers Incheon, Gimpo and Gimhae |
| **Registration** | Required for aircraft over **250 g**, even recreationally. The portal requires a **Korean mobile number** for SMS verification — a genuine barrier for visitors |
| **Under 250 g** | No registration needed to fly recreationally. **Every airspace restriction still applies** |
| **Altitude** | 150 m ceiling |
| **Night flight** | Requires separate permit, up to a **30-day** processing time |
| **Aerial filming** | A **separate** authorisation from flight approval, plus site permission |

**Practical advice if you want drone footage:** the realistic options are designated recreational drone parks (Hangang Drone Park, or the 29 ultra-light aircraft zones) or hiring a licensed Korean operator. Attempting the permit process independently as a visitor is close to impossible. Do **not** attempt it near the DMZ, over Seoul, or within 9.3 km of any airport.

**If you are not bringing a drone, ignore all of the above** — it is here because the penalties are severe enough to be worth knowing.

## 👕 Dress and the hanbok option

**Practical:** late October is 12–16°C by day, 6–10°C at night. Layers, a real warm jacket, gloves. See [[Korea 2026 — Weather and Packing|the packing note]] for the full list.

**Hanbok rental** — roughly ₩30,000–40,000 (US$22–30), pressed and ready in about twenty minutes. Wearing hanbok **waives admission** to Gyeongbokgung and several other palaces, which makes it the cheapest upgrade in Seoul rather than a costume expense. Most rental shops are 5–10 minutes from the palaces. Bring your passport.

## 🏥 Health and safety

| | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **Tap water** | Safe to drink in Seoul, Busan and Gyeongju. Bottled is more common anyway |
| **Pharmacies** | Ubiquitous. Many are open late; look for the green cross |
| **Cuts and grazes** | Available without prescription |
| **Stomach upset** | Normal traveller's risk with new food. Carry rehydration salts |
| **Emergency** | **112** — police, fire and ambulance, nationwide |
| **Tourist assistance** | **1330** — 24-hour multilingual helpline |

> [!tip] Carry your passport's photo page on your phone
> If it is ever lost, a digital copy speeds up an embassy replacement considerably. Not a substitute for the document, but it turns a same-day disaster into an afternoon one.

## ♿ Accessibility

Seoul's metro is largely step-free at major stations and increasingly so generally. **Gyeongju's historic centre and Gamcheon Culture Village are not accessible** — Gamcheon in particular is stairs with no alternative route. If that affects you, say so before departure and the Day 6 cut changes shape.
