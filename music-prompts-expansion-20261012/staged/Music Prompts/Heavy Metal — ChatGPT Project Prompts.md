## 1. Start the project

```text
Let’s start exploring Heavy Metal together. Read the project instructions and uploaded sources so we can continue from the work already prepared.

Give me an overview of its history, main styles, classic albums, and current discoveries. Start with traditional Heavy Metal while explaining its place in the wider metal family. Distinguish verified research, editorial recommendations, and gaps.

Recommend three contrasting albums to begin with, explain what to notice in each, and ask me one useful question about what I already enjoy. Do not assume I have listened to or rated any album.
```

## 2. History in chapters

```text
Create a sourced chapter plan for Heavy Metal from precursors through the 1970s, NWOBHM, international scenes, later decades, and traditional revival. Start the first chapter. ```

## 16. Build a full recording dossier

```text
Develop a sourced dossier for [artist / recording / edition] in my Heavy Metal project. Verify exact title, original date, format, track order, personnel, label, recording/mixing/mastering credits, and edition differences. Explain composition and context with attention to riff and hook construction, vocal phrasing, guitar harmony, bass movement, solos, and arrangement. Separate artist statements, attributed criticism, editorial interpretation, and my supplied reaction [optional feedback]. Include three listening questions, verified official links or labeled searches, and unresolved metadata. Do not claim direct listening or fill personal scores. Default to traditional heavy metal; label speed, doom, power, progressive, and other neighboring or hybrid recordings explicitly.
```

## 17. Choose a route through one discography

```text
Build a manageable listening route through [artist] for my Heavy Metal project. Verify original releases and identify meaningful changes in personnel, composition, or presentation. Choose three entry recordings for [goal or sound], explain why each belongs, and list two later branches to explore. Distinguish chronological order from the recommended route; label demos, EPs, live recordings, compilations, and reissues. Default to traditional heavy metal; label speed, doom, power, progressive, and other neighboring or hybrid recordings explicitly. Use this project’s explicit feedback only and leave listening status unknown unless I reported it.
```

## 18. Prepare a track-by-track listening companion

```text
Create a listening companion for [artist / recording / exact edition]. Verify the original track order and separate bonus tracks. For each original track give one short question about riff and hook construction, vocal phrasing, guitar harmony, bass movement, solos, and arrangement, based on sourced analysis or my notes [optional notes]. Identify track-specific statements as documented or interpretive. If evidence supports only album-level discussion, give album-level questions instead of invented song details. Do not fabricate timestamps, transcriptions, direct listening, or my reactions. Finish with a blank three-question feedback form.
```

## 19. Artwork, credits, and edition identity

```text
Research the artwork and presentation of [artist / recording] in my Heavy Metal project. Verify the original cover, credited artist or photographer, design, and documented later variants. Explain confirmed artistic intent separately from visual interpretation; preserve conflicting or unavailable credits. Link responsible image sources without treating a product thumbnail as evidence of an edition’s contents. Compare original, remastered, remixed, or expanded versions where relevant; do not infer audio quality from format, price, or cover art. Produce a short illustrated-note plan, without claiming images were downloaded or attached.
```

## 20. Use my feedback to choose the next experiment

```text

Choose two close matches and one deliberate contrast, explaining which preference each tests. Verify recording identities, original years, formats, and official links. Default to traditional heavy metal; label speed, doom, power, progressive, and other neighboring or hybrid recordings explicitly.
