## The kind of trip worth building

For your first China draft, I suggest three bases with different purposes: Beijing for major historic sights, Xi’an for the Terracotta Warriors, and Shanghai for city photography and unhurried browsing. The point of this route is to give you both recognizable landmarks and room for the everyday experiences you enjoy in Japan and Korea. It should leave time to sit in a café, find a meal without rushing, and return to a promising street when the light changes.

Fourteen nights is a useful comparison scenario rather than a requirement. It gives two internal transfer days enough space without turning each city into a checklist. If leave is shorter, reduce the number of bases before reducing every stay. Beijing and Shanghai alone would make a coherent ten-night version. If landscapes are the main reason for going, rebuild the route around them instead of adding a mountain destination to an already full city itinerary.

## A 14-night starting scenario

| Local days | Overnight base | Nights | Suggested focus |

| 1–5 | Beijing | 5 | Arrival recovery, historic sights, neighborhood walks, one Great Wall day |
| 6–8 | Xi’an | 3 | Transfer, Terracotta Warriors, food and flexible city time |
| 9–14 | Shanghai | 6 | Transfer, riverfront photography, cafés, browsing, optional excursion |
| 15 | Depart Shanghai | 0 | Departure according to actual international flight |

**5 + 3 + 6 = 14 hotel nights.** Days 6 and 9 are transfer days; sightseeing depends on the chosen service and check-in. Day 1 is arrival in China, not departure from Santiago. Reversing the route is possible. A flight requiring a return to Beijing would need a new night allocation and a protected connection plan.

### Beijing: give the big sights breathing room

The municipal tourism directory identifies the Palace Museum/Forbidden City, Temple of Heaven, and Summer Palace as major sights. I would choose two as priorities, then retain the third as an option. Trying to cover all three, the Wall, and several shopping areas in a short stay would squeeze out the slower photography time. [Beijing municipal tourism directory](https://english.beijing.gov.cn/travellinginbeijing/mustvisitsites/).

Use the arrival day for recovery and a nearby meal. Give one day to the Palace Museum area, one to a Great Wall excursion, and divide the remaining time between a second historic site and neighborhood exploration. The Palace Museum normally closes Mondays (national-holiday exceptions) and sells no same-day tickets; foreign online reservations require passport numbers. Recheck the actual date before making a timed plan. [Official visitor page](https://intl.dpm.org.cn/visit.html?l=en). A neighborhood photo walk can move; a reserved museum visit may not.

Mutianyu is the first Great Wall section I would research for you. Beijing’s official page places it in Huairou and lists public transport with a bus change, as well as cable-car facilities. That makes it a dedicated outing rather than a casual city stop. Compare independent transport with a small-group transfer once dates and walking preferences are known; cable cars and other extras must be checked separately. [Official Mutianyu overview](https://english.beijing.gov.cn/travellinginbeijing/attractions/202603/t20260325_4566115.html).

For hotel research, begin with a central base and convenient metro access. Dongcheng is a candidate search area, not a hotel recommendation. Judge the actual property by its station walk, airport arrival route, room quality, and full-stay cost. Protect a flexible day so weather or fatigue does not force every outing into the first available slot.

### Xi’an: build around one purposeful excursion

The reason to keep Xi’an in this first route is the Terracotta Warriors. The museum’s current visitor instructions require advance real-name reservations and the original identification document used for booking. Its displayed standard adult admission is **CNY 120 per person**, checked 8 October 2026; this is a published admission price, not a dated package quote, and excludes separately priced services. [Museum visitor and ticket instructions](https://www.bmy.com.cn/jingtai/bmyweb/ticketing.html).

I would devote one full planning day to the museum outing, leaving the transfer afternoon and another day for city exploration and meals. The ticket page contains an ambiguous equipment restriction translated as “camera equipment”; verify still-camera and filming rules before taking a substantial camera kit. Do not assume admission permits every form of filming. Its English navigation also links to a reseller while its text names official channels, so verify the actual purchase channel and terms before payment.

Food research should become a short, geographically useful list rather than a catalogue. Noodles, roujiamo, and dumplings are candidate themes to investigate; specific restaurants, serving sizes, protein options, and opening times remain to be checked. A central hotel search can start around the Bell Tower area, with station access and noise checked property by property.

### Shanghai: keep space for your own discoveries

Shanghai is where I would give your café, shopping, and urban photography interests the most time. Its official tourism site describes the Bund along the Huangpu River as a major historic architecture area with an evening tourism scene. I would return to it under different light instead of treating one quick visit as sufficient. No skyline lighting schedule or clear-weather view is guaranteed. [Official Bund overview](https://www.meet-in-shanghai.net/en/nighttime-culture/bund-sightseeing-area-026252/).

Allocate separate blocks to a riverfront walk, café exploration, and browsing, with a museum or indoor block available when outdoor conditions are poor. Jing’an and Xuhui are candidate hotel/search districts for the next round; exact cafés, vintage stores, record shops, and film-related shops need location and access verification before entering the shortlist. Chinese tea is also a potential new interest to explore rather than assuming the Japan matcha programme transfers unchanged.

An excursion to Suzhou or Hangzhou is an option to research, not part of the base itinerary. Choose one only after comparing transport and your preferred scenery. Keep the final day before departure light enough to pack, return purchases to the hotel, and check the actual airport transfer.

## Two different directions

**Chengdu alternative:** replace Xi’an and reallocate to Beijing 4 + Chengdu 4 + Shanghai 6. Research tea culture, regional food, and panda-related attractions before choosing activities. This gives up the Terracotta Warriors. Transport, attraction suitability, and specific businesses have not been verified in this starter draft.

**Landscape alternative:** Beijing 4 + Zhangjiajie 4 + Shanghai 6 is an exploration candidate. It would need fresh research on park geography, access, weather, walking demands, and internal flights/trains before recommendation. Do not attach a separate scenic region to a two-week itinerary simply because it looks close on a country map.

## Choosing a season and protecting the pace

Begin by comparing an April–May window with a later-October window. These are research candidates, not a weather forecast or a selected year. Check each city’s climate, holiday calendar, and the importance of any landscape excursion before recommending exact weeks. China is too varied for one nationwide “best month” claim.

Holiday timing deserves an early check: the government’s September 2026 notice identifies National Day’s **1–7 October 2026** break as a major tourism spending window. I would compare dates outside the main holiday if flexibility allows. Future years require their own published holiday schedules; 2026 dates do not settle 2027 arrangements. [Government holiday tourism notice, 22 September 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202609/22/content_WS6ab22197c6d00ca5f9a0d4fc.html).

## Transport and research order

Compare Beijing–Xi’an rail and Xi’an–Shanghai rail versus flying when dates become useful. Record the exact stations or airports, baggage terms, city transfers, and door-to-door time. This draft does not claim train durations, timetables, booking windows, or fares.

China Railway’s English FAQ accepts valid foreign passports for real-name tickets and requires the identification used for purchase when boarding. Prepare the identity details before ticket research becomes urgent. [12306 official FAQ](https://www.12306.cn/en/faq.html?item=2).

For flights, compare a multi-city Santiago → Beijing / Shanghai → Santiago itinerary with equivalent round-trip alternatives. Confirm San Fernando–SCL transport and any transit requirements separately. For hotels, record actual China check-in/out dates, adults, rooms, taxes, and complete stay totals. Tours should follow the chosen route and priorities. Keep all exploratory prices in research notes until an actual reservation is supplied.
