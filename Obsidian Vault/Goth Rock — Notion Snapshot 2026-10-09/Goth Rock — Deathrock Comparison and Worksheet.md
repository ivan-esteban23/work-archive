## What separates them

- **45 Grave — keyboard-colored punk attack with horror-show phrasing.** The credits list two keyboard players alongside the core band, and reception sources repeatedly frame the record as deathrock's most song-oriented document. Editorial listening suggestion: start with "Evil" and test how much the recurring refrain contributes to catchiness for you.
- **Kommunity FK — live-in-the-room abrasion.** The documented recording method (live takes, minimal overdubs) and a lineup without a keyboard-heavy production suggest a rawer texture than the other two. Editorial suggestion: choose an original-album track and follow one repeating instrumental figure.
- **Cinema Strange — theatrical revival arrangements.** German Wikipedia describes hectically played music with bass as a dark, grooving walking line equal to or above the guitar. Lucas Lanthier has said the band moved away from wanting to write "catchy deathrock anthems" (artist statement, cited by German Wikipedia) — directly relevant to your catchy-rhythm preference. Editorial suggestion: start with "Dead Eyes Open" and test vocal theatricality against weight.

All three records are year-precision. Treat editions separately: reissue track orders and bonus material are not the original sequence.
## Historical position (sourced)

- Wikipedia's deathrock article names Christian Death, Kommunity FK, 45 Grave, and the Super Heroines as the pioneering Los Angeles bands, and notes the term was largely applied retrospectively. [Deathrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/deathrock)
- Kommunity FK formed in 1978 and "helped establish what came to be as the deathrock scene in Los Angeles" (Wikipedia). 45 Grave appeared on the 1981 *Hell Comes to Your House* compilation alongside Christian Death.
- Cinema Strange formed in 1994 in Southern California; their 2001 Wave-Gotik-Treffen performance was a breakthrough, and German Wikipedia situates this second album as the record where the band pivots from revival deathrock toward dark wave and art music.
- Revival framing: Wikipedia dates the first deathrock revival to the 1998 founding of the Release the Bats club night in Long Beach; [post-punk.com](http://post-punk.com) credits Cinema Strange's self-titled debut with spearheading the 2000s revival (attributed opinion).

## Corrections and open questions

- The 1983 original of *Sleep in Safety* is on Enigma Records; Frontier Records is associated with 45 Grave's later *Pick Your Poison* (2012). The dossier's listening route uses an expanded platform edition, not the original sequence.
- Kommunity FK's "recorded 1981, released 1983" note is single-sourced.
- The Cleopatra CD year for *The Vision and the Voice* conflicts: 1993 (Wikipedia) versus 1994/1998 (band discography).
