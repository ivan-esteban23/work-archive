## 👖 Koenji — my first new pick for vintage

Start with a short section of **Pal Shopping Street**, then choose one or two SAFARI branches rather than touring them all. SAFARI’s official directory separates vintage clothing, Ralph Lauren/RRL, outdoor clothing, and European vintage across its Koenji stores. Choose the branch by your style. Selected or collectible vintage can cost more than ordinary secondhand clothing; compare condition and fit before buying.

**Route fit:** an alternative Tokyo half-day on 30 March or 7 April. Koenji and Kichijoji lie along the JR Chuo corridor, but each merits its own browsing time. Pick one if you want a relaxed outing and check which train stops at your station on the travel date.

[SAFARI official store directory](https://e-safari.co.jp/) · [Official Koenji shopping guide](https://www.gotokyo.org/book/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/AO_2603_suginamichuo_low_EN.pdf) · [Maps: Koenji Pal Shopping Street](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Koenji+Pal+Shopping+Street+Tokyo)

## ☕ Shimokitazawa — the easiest vintage-and-coffee combination

The guide already includes Shimokitazawa. GO TOKYO identifies **New York Joe Exchange** for secondhand clothing and **Reload** for shops and cafés. Browse after lunch, then consider LIGHT UP COFFEE’s Shimokitazawa branch at **2-29-12 Daita**. The coffee shop sits on the Setagaya-Daita side, so check the walk rather than assuming it is beside your clothing store.

**Route fit:** substitute for another optional shopping session. Keep one main vintage purchase in mind; do not increase the shopping budget to fit extra shops.

[Official neighborhood guide](https://www.gotokyo.org/en/destinations/western-tokyo/shimokitazawa/index.html) · [LIGHT UP COFFEE branches](https://lightupcoffee.com/pages/store) · [Maps: New York Joe Shimokitazawa](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=New+York+Joe+Exchange+Shimokitazawa+Tokyo) · [Maps: coffee branch](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=LIGHT+UP+COFFEE+Shimokitazawa+2-29-12+Daita)

## 🌳 Kichijoji — shops, coffee, and greenery

SAFARI lists **Nanaibashi** and **Nakamichi-dori** shops for American vintage. LIGHT UP COFFEE’s Kichijoji branch is at **4-13-15 Kichijoji Honmachi**, and its official Japanese page lists a three-coffee tasting option. Combine selected shopping with a coffee stop and the existing Inokashira Park option; plot the entrances before deciding the order.

**Route fit:** develop the guide’s existing Kichijoji alternative on 30 March or 7 April. Return with time to pack. Café English/Japanese pages currently differ on hours and prices, so use current local notices before visiting; no fixed 2027 price is assumed.

## 📚 Daikanyama — books, design, and a polished café stop

Browse **DAIKANYAMA TSUTAYA BOOKS / T-SITE**, then consider **IVY PLACE**, a café/bar/dining venue at the complex. This is my pick when you want attractive surroundings and books rather than a clothing hunt. Check the current menu, reservation options, and prices before choosing a meal.

**Route fit:** the existing 29 March free block before Nakameguro. Drop this addition if blossom conditions make another photo location the priority.

## ✒️ Kuramae — stationery and chocolate

**Kakimori** is the writing-focused shop to consider: notebooks, pens, inks, and papers, including order-made notebooks. If customization is your priority, confirm turnaround and any booking requirement before going. **Dandelion Chocolate Factory & Café Kuramae** makes a separate café stop for chocolate drinks or sweets; check its current menu.

**Route fit:** pair with an Asakusa outing only if that day has space, or replace another optional Tokyo half-day. This is a stationery-and-café choice rather than a main thrift-shopping destination.

### ☕ A record-listening café

**RECOCO Shimokitazawa** offers a hands-on vinyl-listening café experience at **2-9-2 Kitazawa, ARISTO Shimokitazawa B1F**. It is a café stop; its site does not establish it as a used-record shop. Current rules include an entry charge plus one drink, cashless payment, no seat reservations, and a 90-minute limit when busy. Check the current menu and fees before going. Do not bring your newly purchased records in for listening: its published rules prohibit outside records.

### 💿 Before buying

- **Vinyl:** ask to inspect the disc and sleeve, understand the shop’s grading, and check missing inserts or an obi strip if completeness matters. Ask whether preview listening is available. Keep records supported and protected from heat or bending in luggage.
- **Movies:** check the exact edition’s region code, audio tracks, and subtitles against your player before paying. Do not assume Japanese releases have English or Spanish subtitles, or that a collector’s edition works on your home player.
- **Posters/scripts:** ask whether the item is original or a reproduction, inspect folds and wear, and request suitable protective packaging. Do not fold a poster simply to fit your suitcase.
- **Budget:** music and film souvenirs share the existing US$300 shopping/miscellaneous allowance. Pick a personal spending cap before browsing; collectible originals can be expensive.
